## Why the book needs *two* type theories, not one

Chapter 2 of the thesis fixed a single core calculus — $\Pi$, $\mathbb{N}$, $\mathrm{Id}$ — and a single notion of definitional equality, the one generated by $\beta$-reduction and the computation rules. That calculus is *intensional*: the identity type $\mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,N)$ is a genuine data type you have to eliminate with the operator $J$, and a proof of $\mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,N)$ never automatically makes $M$ and $N$ interchangeable during typechecking. That's exactly what makes it decidable — typechecking reduces terms to normal form and compares them syntactically, full stop.

But Chapter 1 already flagged the price of that decidability: ordinary mathematical practice constantly needs things intensional equality can't give you — two pointwise-equal functions being *the* equal function, or a quotient construction identifying elements outright. The tempting fix is the **equality reflection rule**:

$$\dfrac{\Gamma \vdash P : \mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,N)}{\Gamma \vdash M = N : \sigma} \qquad (\mathrm{Id\text{-}DefEq})$$

Once you have *any* proof $P$ that $M$ and $N$ are propositionally equal, they become definitionally equal — interchangeable everywhere, silently, without having to write $J$ or $\mathrm{Subst}$ at every use site. This is enormously convenient. It is also the single rule this chapter exists to interrogate. Chapter 3's job is to give the reader two precise, comparable formal systems — call them $\mathrm{TT_I}$ (intensional) and $\mathrm{TT_E}$ (extensional) — that differ *only* in whether this rule is present, and then prove two theorems that jointly settle which one you should actually build a proof assistant or a compiler around:

1. $\mathrm{TT_E}$'s typechecking is **undecidable** (§3.2.2).
2. $\mathrm{TT_E}$ is nevertheless **conservative** over $\mathrm{TT_I}$ extended with functional extensionality and uniqueness of identity (§3.2.5) — so intensional type theory loses nothing as far as which types are inhabited, provided you're willing to add those two extensional concepts as primitives (which the rest of the thesis then shows how to eliminate again, via models).

If you are designing a dependently-typed kernel — which is exactly the standing project behind this note — this chapter is close to the single most load-bearing piece of theory in the whole thesis: it is the formal argument for *why* every production dependent type checker (Lean's kernel, Coq's, Agda's) is built on the $\mathrm{TT_I}$ side of this fence, and for what you're allowed to assume you haven't lost by doing so.

## $\mathrm{TT_I}$ and $\mathrm{TT_E}$ as competing formulations

### The intensional side, $\mathrm{TT_I}$

Section 3.1 first tools up the intensional calculus with derived combinators so that later comparisons have something to point at. From the identity-elimination operator $J$ one defines **Leibniz substitution**:

$$\mathrm{Subst}_{\Gamma,\Phi}(M,M',P,N) : \Phi[M']\quad\text{given } N : \Phi[M],\ P : \mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,M')$$

with computation rule $\mathrm{Subst}_{\Gamma,\Phi}(M,M,\mathrm{Refl}(M),N) = N$. This is the operator that actually "replaces $M$ by $M'$" inside a type — but, crucially, it *leaves a mark in the term*. Where $\mathrm{TT_E}$ will let you write $N : \Phi[M']$ directly, $\mathrm{TT_I}$ forces you to write $\mathrm{Subst}_{\Gamma,\Phi}(M,M',P,N) : \Phi[M']$, carrying the proof $P$ along as data. $\mathrm{Sym}$, $\mathrm{Trans}$, and $\mathrm{Resp}$ (compatibility with application) are all just instances of $\mathrm{Subst}$.

Two further constants get bolted on, because $J$ alone can't derive them (proved later, in Chapter 5, via the groupoid model):

- **$\mathrm{IdUni}$** (uniqueness of identity proofs, UIP): a family of constants $\mathrm{IdUni}_\sigma(M,P) : \mathrm{Id}_{\mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,M)}(P,\mathrm{Refl}(M))$, with computation rule $\mathrm{IdUni}_\sigma(M,\mathrm{Refl}(M)) = \mathrm{Refl}(\mathrm{Refl}(M))$. Hofmann shows (Prop. 3.1.1) UIP is actually *derivable without any axiom* at $0$, $1$, $\mathbb{N}$, $\Sigma$-types, and $\mathrm{Id}$-types themselves, given a universe — the axiom is only needed to close the gap at universes and at abstract type formers. He also notes in passing (§3.1.2.2) that Coquand-style pattern matching for dependent types trivially *defines* $\mathrm{IdUni}$ at every type — a fact whose consequences detonate later in Chapter 5 (pattern matching turns out to be a *non-conservative* extension of $\mathrm{TT_I}$, precisely because it smuggles in UIP for free).
- **$\mathrm{Ext}$** (functional extensionality, "Turner's solution"): $\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma,\Phi}(U,V,P) : \mathrm{Id}_{\Pi x:\sigma.\Phi}(U,V)$ given $P : \mathrm{Id}_\Phi(Ux,Vx)$ pointwise. Hofmann is explicit that this is *not* free: adding $\mathrm{Ext}$ as a bare constant with no reduction behavior wrecks N-canonicity, because a term like $\mathrm{Subst}_{\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N},[f]\mathbb{N}}(\mathrm{Ext}(P), 0)$ sits in the empty context at type $\mathbb{N}$ but simply doesn't reduce to a numeral — it's stuck on an axiom with no computational content. (It *is* propositionally equal to a numeral, via $J$, but propositional equality is exactly the notion that's supposed to be doing extra work here — you can't get it for free by reflection, because you're intensional.) This non-canonicity is the concrete "what breaks" if you try to shortcut Chapter 5's syntactic models and just axiomatize extensionality directly.

Formally, for the rest of the chapter, $\mathrm{TT_I}$ *means*: the core calculus $\mathrm{TT}$ plus $\mathrm{IdUni}$, $\mathrm{Ext}$, and with $J$ replaced by the equivalent but more primitive rule pair

$$\dfrac{\Gamma,x{:}\sigma \vdash \Phi[x] \quad \Gamma \vdash M_1,M_2 {:} \sigma \quad \Gamma \vdash P {:} \mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M_1,M_2) \quad \Gamma \vdash N {:} \Phi[M_1]}{\Gamma \vdash \mathrm{Subst}_{\Gamma,\Phi}(M_1,M_2,P,N) : \Phi[M_2]} \quad(\mathrm{Leibniz})$$

$$\Gamma \vdash \mathrm{Subst}_{\Gamma,\Phi}(M,M,\mathrm{Refl}(M),N) = N : \Phi[M] \quad (\mathrm{Leibniz\text{-}Comp})$$

This swap is legitimate — Streicher had already observed that in the presence of UIP, $J$ is definable from $\mathrm{Subst}$ alone (you build the motive $\Psi[x] := \exists p{:}\mathrm{Id}(M,x).\,\Phi[M,x,p]$ and push a UIP-supplied witness through it) — and it's convenient because $\mathrm{Subst}$, unlike $J$, is the operator that actually appears throughout the rest of the chapter's model constructions.

### The extensional side, $\mathrm{TT_E}$

$\mathrm{TT_E}$ is $\mathrm{TT}$ with $J$/its computation rule *removed* and replaced by exactly two rules:

$$\dfrac{\Gamma \vdash \sigma \quad \Gamma \vdash M{:}\sigma \quad \Gamma \vdash N{:}\sigma \quad \Gamma \vdash P{:}\mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,N)}{\Gamma \vdash M = N : \sigma} \quad(\mathrm{Id\text{-}DefEq})$$

$$\dfrac{\Gamma \vdash \sigma \quad \Gamma \vdash M{:}\sigma \quad \Gamma \vdash N{:}\sigma \quad \Gamma \vdash P{:}\mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,N)}{\Gamma \vdash P = \mathrm{Refl}(M) : \mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,N)} \quad(\mathrm{Id\text{-}Uni})$$

plus a $\Pi$-$\eta$ rule. Note the dependency: $\mathrm{Id\text{-}Uni}$ only *typechecks* because $\mathrm{Id\text{-}DefEq}$ already let you conclude $M = N : \sigma$ from $P$'s existence, so $\mathrm{Refl}(M) : \mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,N)$ becomes well-typed. Once you have both, $J$, $\mathrm{IdUni}$, and $\mathrm{Ext}$ all become *definable* — in fact trivial, since every proof of every identity type just *is* $\mathrm{Refl}$ up to definitional equality (Hofmann gives the one-line definition $J(M,N_1,N_2,P) := M[x:=N_1]$). This is what makes $\mathrm{TT_E}$ look, on paper, unambiguously nicer: no elimination-operator bookkeeping, a single equality notion, and a much closer match to how mathematicians actually reason ("if $x=y$ then obviously..."). The rest of the chapter is spent showing why this is a mirage.

One notational trap the thesis flags explicitly (§3.2.1): Troelstra and van Dalen's "intensional" $\mathrm{ML_0^i}$ is *not* the same system as this thesis's $\mathrm{TT_I}$ — it's much closer to $\mathrm{TT_E}$ (it has $\mathrm{Id\text{-}DefEq}$, just with type equality restricted to syntactic identity). Their "extensional" $\mathrm{ML_0}$ is the one that matches $\mathrm{TT_E}$ here. Worth keeping straight if you ever cross-reference the two texts.

**Lean grounding.** Lean's kernel is a working artifact of exactly this choice. `isDefEq` never performs equality reflection: a term `h : a = b` (an `Eq` proof) does *not* make the kernel treat `a` and `b` as defeq — you must `rw`/`▸`/`Eq.mpr` through `h` explicitly, and that rewriting shows up as an actual subterm, precisely the way $\mathrm{Subst}$ leaves a mark. What Lean's kernel *does* reflect definitionally is proof-irrelevance for `Prop` (any two proofs of the same proposition are defeq) — that's a much weaker, and crucially *decidable*, form of extensionality; it corresponds to the thesis's proof-irrelevance concept (studied in Chapter 4, not this chapter) rather than to full $\mathrm{Id\text{-}DefEq}$ reflection on arbitrary `Eq`. Lean's `Eq.mpr`/`▸`, `rfl`, `Eq.refl` are the concrete surface syntax for $\mathrm{Subst}$, $\mathrm{Refl}$, and — when you invoke `Subsingleton.elim`/`proof_irrel`-style lemmas — a definable fragment of $\mathrm{IdUni}$.

```lean
-- Subst, by hand: rewriting inside a motive, leaving an explicit mark
example {α : Type} (P : α → Prop) (a b : α) (h : a = b) (p : P a) : P b :=
  h ▸ p   -- literally Subst, not silent reflection

-- what TTE would let you write instead (and Lean's kernel refuses):
-- if h : a = b, treat "P a" and "P b" as the *same type*, no ▸ needed
```

**Rust grounding.** There's no proof-relevant identity type in Rust, but the shape of the distinction shows up in how a typestate/marker-based API forces you to *carry evidence* rather than silently coercing. A `Sorted<T>` wrapper obtained by an explicit `fn sort(v: Vec<T>) -> Sorted<T>` is the $\mathrm{TT_I}$ move: the compiler tracks a syntactic marker (the wrapper type), and nothing lets you treat `Vec<T>` and `Sorted<T>` as the same type just because you happen to have a runtime proof they contain the same elements in order. `unsafe { std::mem::transmute(v) }` is the (unsound, in general) $\mathrm{Id\text{-}DefEq}$ move — declare two types "the same" on the strength of an external, unchecked guarantee, with no trace left in the type system.

## Undecidability of typechecking in extensional type theory

Here is the chapter's first hard result, and it is worth internalizing exactly *why* it holds, because the intuition ("losing information is bad") isn't quite the mechanism.

First, the easy reductions (§3.2.2): all of $\mathrm{TT_E}$'s judgement forms collapse into one another. Definitional equality decides typechecking, because $\Gamma \vdash \mathrm{Refl}_\sigma(M) : \mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,N)$ holds iff $\Gamma \vdash M = N : \sigma$. Typehood reduces to typechecking, because $\Gamma \vdash \sigma$ iff $\Gamma \vdash \mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,M)$ for some (any) $M{:}\sigma$. So it suffices to show *one* of these is undecidable, and the thesis targets definitional equality.

The proof (§3.2.2) is a direct encoding of self-application, not a generic appeal to Rice's theorem. Fix a Gödel numbering of Turing machines and define

$$f(e,t) := \begin{cases}1 & \text{if } e \text{ applied to itself halts in} < t \text{ steps with result } 0\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$

which is primitive recursive (the number of steps to *check* is linear in $e,t$, even though whether it halts at all is undecidable), hence numeral-wise representable by some $\hat f : \mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ in the core theory. Two facts about $\hat f$:

- If $e$ applied to itself halts with a result $\ne 0$, then $f(e,\cdot)$ is constantly $0$, and — crucially — type theory can *prove* this by primitive recursion/case analysis on the finitely many steps before halting: $t{:}\mathbb{N} \vdash \mathrm{Id}_\mathbb{N}(\hat f\,\hat e\,t, 0)$ is inhabited. Via $\mathrm{Id\text{-}DefEq}$ this becomes the *definitional* equation $t{:}\mathbb{N} \vdash \hat f\,\hat e\,t = 0 : \mathbb{N}$.
- If $e$ applied to itself halts *with* result $0$, that equation provably fails (it would contradict $f$'s own definition at the halting step).

So: if definitional equality in $\mathrm{TT_E}$ were decidable, you could build a machine that, given $e$, checks whether $t{:}\mathbb{N} \vdash \hat f\,\hat e\,t = 0 : \mathbb{N}$ holds and outputs $0$ if so, $1$ otherwise — and this machine *always halts on every input*, because the equation is a first-order syntactic question, not a semantic halting question. Feed this machine its own Gödel number $e_0$: by construction its self-application terminates, but whichever output it produces contradicts the case analysis above. Contradiction. Hence:

> **Theorem 3.2.1.** In $\mathrm{TT_E}$, definitional equality, typechecking, and typehood are pairwise equivalent and jointly undecidable.

The engine of the proof is precisely [[Definitional-Versus-Propositional-Equality#The equality reflection rule|the equality reflection rule]]: it's what lets a semantic fact ("$e$ doesn't halt with result $0$, provably, by exhaustive case analysis over finitely many steps") get absorbed into the *syntactic* judgement `$\hat f\,\hat e\,t = 0$`, so that deciding syntactic equality becomes at least as hard as deciding the semantic fact. Take $\mathrm{Id\text{-}DefEq}$ away, and definitional equality stays purely syntax-directed (strong normalization + syntactic comparison of normal forms), decidable by construction — that decision procedure is precisely what Chapter 2 already proved exists for the intensional core calculus.

**What this buys, concretely, for a kernel.** This is the theorem that answers, once and for all, "why doesn't my trusted kernel just let `rfl`-by-proof-existence stand in for arbitrary equality proofs?" — because doing so makes the kernel's most basic operation, `isDefEq`, semidecidable at best. Every real dependent type checker's decidability guarantee — Lean's, Coq's, Agda's — traces back to *not* taking this rule. It is the direct type-theoretic analogue of why an SMT-backed verifier keeps proof search (undecidable in general, or at best a decidable fragment like linear arithmetic) firmly outside the trusted kernel, and only lets *checking a supplied certificate* (a $\mathrm{Subst}$-annotated term, or an SMT proof witness) back in — proof *search* can be as semantically rich and undecidable as you like, so long as proof *checking* stays syntax-directed and total.

```python
# sketch: TTE-style defeq-checker would need to decide semantic facts, not just
# compare normal forms -- this is exactly where it goes semidecidable.
def defeq_TTI(t1, t2):           # intensional: purely syntactic
    return normalize(t1) == normalize(t2)   # terminates: strong normalization

def defeq_TTE(t1, t2, ctx):      # extensional: must also check "is there some
    if normalize(t1) == normalize(t2):      # proof P : Id(t1, t2) derivable?"
        return True
    return exists_proof_of_identity(ctx, t1, t2)   # semidecidable at best
```

## The stripping map between $\mathrm{TT_E}$ and $\mathrm{TT_I}$

Given the undecidability result, real extensional systems (Nuprl is the thesis's running example) work with *derivations*, not bare terms — but that raises the natural question: can a $\mathrm{TT_E}$ derivation always be *translated* into an intensional one? Section 3.2.3 sets up the machinery to ask this precisely, via a **stripping map** $|\cdot|$ from $\mathrm{TT_I}$ to $\mathrm{TT_E}$: erase every $\mathrm{TT_I}$-specific term former, replacing it by whatever it "morally" computes to in an extensional world where such bookkeeping is unnecessary:

$$|\mathrm{Subst}_{\Gamma,\Phi}(M_1,M_2,P,N)| := |N| \qquad |\mathrm{IdUni}_\sigma(M,P)| := \mathrm{Refl}_{\mathrm{Id}_{|\sigma|}(|M|,|M|)}(\mathrm{Refl}_{|\sigma|}(|M|)) \qquad |\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma,\Phi}(U,V,P)| := \mathrm{Refl}_{\Pi x{:}|\sigma|.|\Phi|}(|U|)$$

extended homomorphically to everything else (a term with no $\mathrm{Subst}$/$\mathrm{IdUni}$/$\mathrm{Ext}$ just strips to itself). This is nothing but an **erasure pass**, in the compiler-construction sense: $\mathrm{Subst}$ is compile-time proof plumbing that has no runtime/extensional content, so it's simply deleted (replaced by the thing it was carrying evidence about).

The soundness direction is easy and the chapter states it as Prop. 3.2.2: **if $\Gamma \vdash_I J$ then $|\Gamma| \vdash_E |J|$** for every judgement $J$ — every $\mathrm{TT_I}$ derivation strips down to a valid $\mathrm{TT_E}$ derivation. (The one interesting case is $\mathrm{Ext}$: given $x{:}\sigma \vdash_E P : \mathrm{Id}_\Phi(Ux,Vx)$, reflection gives $Ux=Vx:\Phi$ definitionally, so by congruence $\lambda x.Ux = \lambda x.Vx$, and $\eta$ then upgrades this to $U=V$ at function type — exactly what $|\mathrm{Ext}(U,V,P)| := \mathrm{Refl}(U)$ needs to be well-typed.)

The interesting direction is the converse — is $|\cdot|$ **surjective**? Given an arbitrary $\mathrm{TT_E}$ derivation, can you always find *some* $\mathrm{TT_I}$ term that strips to it? Call this property **Inv**. It is *false* for plain $\mathrm{TT_I}$: the judgement

$$x{:}\mathbb{N} \vdash_E x = \mathrm{Suc}^x(0) : \mathbb{N}$$

(true in $\mathrm{TT_E}$ by induction plus reflection, since a proof of $\mathrm{Id}_\mathbb{N}(x, \mathrm{Suc}^x(0))$ exists) has no $\mathrm{TT_I}$-derivable counterpart with matching stripping — the only way a $\mathrm{Subst}$-headed term can ever become definitionally equal to something is via $\mathrm{Leibniz\text{-}Comp}$, and that rule only fires when the two sides of the equality proof are syntactically identical, which erases the outermost $\mathrm{Subst}$ without adding any new equation. There's no intensional term with the shape "$x$, but justified by an inductive proof" — you either genuinely reduce to $x$, or you don't.

Hofmann's fix (§3.2.4) is to add exactly one new term former, $\triangleleft(M,N,P)$, read as "*means* $M$ but is *written* $N$, justified by $P{:}\mathrm{Id}(M,N)$":

$$\dfrac{\Gamma\vdash M,N{:}\sigma \quad \Gamma\vdash P{:}\mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,N)}{\Gamma \vdash \triangleleft_\sigma(M,N,P){:}\sigma}\ (\triangleleft\text{-}\mathrm{Form}) \qquad \dfrac{\Gamma\vdash\triangleleft_\sigma(M,N,P){:}\sigma}{\Gamma\vdash \triangleleft_\sigma(M,N,P)=N{:}\sigma}\ (\triangleleft\text{-}\mathrm{Eq})$$

with stripping $|\triangleleft(M,N,P)| := |M|$. This is a purely definitional extension (comparable to adding a named multiplication symbol that's definitionally equal to its primitive-recursive encoding) — but it's exactly the missing hook: in the counterexample above, $\triangleleft(x, \mathrm{Suc}^x(0), P)$ *is* definitionally equal to $\mathrm{Suc}^x(0)$ (by $\triangleleft$-Eq) while stripping to $x$. **Theorem 3.2.4**: with $\triangleleft$ added, $|\cdot|$ *is* surjective — every $\mathrm{TT_E}$ derivation lifts to a $\mathrm{TT}^\triangleleft$ derivation with matching stripping (the interesting cases in the induction are exactly $\mathrm{Id\text{-}DefEq}$, $\mathrm{Id\text{-}Uni}$, and $\eta$, each simulated by inserting a $\triangleleft$ around the point where reflection or $\eta$ was used).

**Rust grounding.** The stripping map is a proof-erasure compiler pass, and $\triangleleft$ is precisely a "trusted cast, annotated with its justification" — think of it as the typed analogue of a `transmute` that *carries its safety proof as a first-class value* rather than discharging it via `unsafe`:

```rust
// TTI term with proof-carrying substitution -- Subst leaves a mark
struct Proven<T> { value: T, _justified_by: EqProof }

// stripping: erase the proof, keep the runtime payload
fn strip<T>(p: Proven<T>) -> T { p.value }

// ⊲(M, N, P): "the value is M, but for typing purposes it's stamped N,
// justified by P" -- definitionally reduces to N, strips to M
struct Cast<T> { written_as: T, meant: T, proof: EqProof }
```

**Lean grounding.** The stripping map is what proof-erasure/extraction does when you compile a Lean term down to executable code: `Eq.mpr`/`▸`-nodes vanish (they carry no runtime information — this is definitional-equality-as-compiler-directive, not data), while the underlying computational content survives. $\triangleleft$ has no direct Lean analogue as a primitive, but it's the same idea behind `cast : α = β → α → β` combined with remembering *why* the cast was justified, rather than performing an unchecked `unsafeCast`.

## Conservativity of $\mathrm{TT_E}$ over $\mathrm{TT_I}$

$\triangleleft$ only answers a question about arbitrary *judgements*. The question that actually matters for "does going extensional buy you more theorems" is narrower and is stated as three properties of increasing strength (all relative to $\mathrm{TT_I}$, i.e. with $\mathrm{Ext}$ and $\mathrm{IdUni}$ already present):

- **Cons-Ty**: $|\Gamma| \vdash_E |\sigma|\ \mathrm{true}$ implies $\Gamma \vdash_I \sigma\ \mathrm{true}$ (if the *stripped* type is inhabited extensionally, the original intensional type was already inhabited).
- **Cons-Id**: $|\Gamma| \vdash_E |M|=|N| : |\sigma|$ implies $\Gamma \vdash_I \mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,N)\ \mathrm{true}$ — a weaker corollary of Cons-Ty.

These are exactly the properties that let you say: *nothing new becomes provable by switching to the more permissive equational theory* — the extra freedom $\mathrm{TT_E}$ gives you at the level of terms and equations never manifests as extra freedom at the level of *which types have inhabitants*. This is the theorem that licenses treating $\mathrm{TT_I}+\mathrm{Ext}+\mathrm{IdUni}$ as "no less expressive, for the purpose of building things, than $\mathrm{TT_E}$" — the load-bearing justification for the whole thesis's strategy of chasing extensional concepts back into intensional models rather than just adopting reflection.

**Proof idea.** The construction is genuinely elegant even though the write-up is dense (Hofmann apologizes for its "relative complexity and clumsiness" in §3.2.6). Take the term model of $\mathrm{TT_I}$ and **quotient it by propositional equality** — but quotienting a categorical model of type theory is delicate, because identifying two morphisms $f,g : \Gamma\to\Delta$ forces you to also identify any families over $\Delta$ pulled back along $f$ versus along $g$, which in turn forces identification of further contexts, and so on. Hofmann sidesteps the need for a *general* theory of such quotients by giving, by structural induction on the syntax, a concrete notion of **propositional isomorphism** between contexts ($\mathrm{co}_{\Gamma,\Delta}$/$\mathrm{co}_{\Gamma,\Delta}^{-1}$) and between types ($\mathrm{ty}_{\Gamma,\Delta,\sigma,\tau}$/its inverse) — defined to exist *exactly when* $|\Gamma|=|\Delta|$ (resp. $|\sigma|=|\tau|$) holds in $\mathrm{TT_E}$. Identifying types/contexts whenever this isomorphism is defined yields a category $\mathbf{C}$ and a syntactic-category-with-attributes $\mathcal{Q}$ over it (Defs. 3.2.12, 3.2.14) — and $\mathcal{Q}$ turns out to *be a model of $\mathrm{TT_E}$* (Prop. 3.2.17): $\Pi$, $\Sigma$, $\mathbb{N}$, and identity types are all constructed in $\mathcal{Q}$ so that equality reflection and $\mathrm{Id\text{-}Uni}$ hold by fiat (equality in $\mathcal{Q}$ *is* the propositional-isomorphism relation).

Stripping lifts to a map $\mathcal{Q}\to\mathrm{TT_E}$ (the term model), and composing $[\![-]\!]: \mathrm{TT_I}\to\mathcal{Q}$ with this stripping recovers exactly the original stripping map $\mathrm{TT_I}\to\mathrm{TT_E}$ (this is initiality: any two structure-preserving round-trips between the same source and target must agree). Chase the diagram

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    TTI["TT_I term model"] -- "⟦-⟧ (interpret)" --> Q["Q (quotient model)"]
    Q -- "|·| (stripping, lifted)" --> TTE["TT_E term model"]
    TTI -- "|·| (stripping, direct)" --> TTE
```

and Theorem 3.2.5 falls out: if $\Gamma \vdash_I \sigma$ and $|\Gamma| \vdash_E P : |\sigma|$, then $[\![|\Gamma|,P]\!]$ is a section of $[\![\sigma]\!]$ in $\mathcal{Q}$; unwinding the definition of "section of a family" in $\mathcal{Q}$ produces a genuinely intensional witness $P' $ with $\Gamma\vdash_I P' : \sigma$ and $|\Gamma|\vdash_E |P'|=P:|\sigma|$.

Two caveats the thesis is careful to flag, both worth internalizing:

- **The proof is non-constructive.** Building $\mathrm{co}$/$\mathrm{ty}$ uses choice, and $\mathcal{Q}$'s objects are literal *equivalence classes*, so producing the actual intensional witness $P'$ requires choosing a class representative — there's no effective algorithm extracted from this proof (a brute-force search algorithm exists trivially, by Markov's principle, but gives no complexity guarantee tied to the size of the $\mathrm{TT_E}$ derivation).
- **The method is robust to extension**, which is exactly why the thesis bothers to show it twice more: §3.2.6.1 extends Cons-Ty to Nuprl-style extensional quotient types (Theorem 3.2.18, by extending the $\mathrm{ty}$-isomorphisms to act on quotients, via an intensional $\mathrm{Qax}$ witness term standing in for $\mathrm{Q\text{-}E\text{-}Eq}$'s reflection), and §3.2.6.2 extends it to a universe closed under $\Sigma$ and $\mathbb{N}$ (Theorem 3.2.20) — interesting in its own right because $\mathrm{TT_E}$ with universes admits *non-normalizing but well-typed* terms (a self-referential code $d : U$ with $\mathrm{Id}_U(d, \widehat{\to}(d,\lambda x{:}d.d))$ types $(\lambda x{:}D.xx)(\lambda x{:}D.xx)$ where $D := \mathrm{El}(d) = D\to D$), and the conservativity theorem immediately produces a *non-normalizing $\mathrm{TT_I}$ term* as a corollary — a striking fact, since it shows the intensional theory's strong normalization property (proved for the core calculus without universes) genuinely fails once you add both universes and enough extensional apparatus to interpret $\mathrm{TT_E}$'s universe.

**Load-bearing connection to elaboration.** The $\mathrm{co}/\mathrm{ty}$ construction is worth reading as a coercion-insertion problem in disguise: "these two types have the same stripping, therefore treat them as isomorphic, and automatically insert the witnessing conversion at every use site" is structurally the same task an elaborator's unifier performs when it decides two types are "the same up to definitional equality (modulo metavariable instantiation)" and inserts an implicit coercion — except here the target notion of sameness is the coarser "same after erasing proof terms," and the coercion is a full propositional isomorphism rather than a defeq check. If you're building a bidirectional elaborator, this is a genuine example of "equality machinery doing unification's job without naming it as such": the structural recursion defining $\mathrm{ty}$ is exactly a unification algorithm for the "same up to stripping" equivalence.

## Non-conservativity of intensional quotient types over pure type theory

Section 3.2.5 established that $\mathrm{TT_E} $ is conservative *over* $\mathrm{TT_I}$ (with $\mathrm{Ext}$, $\mathrm{IdUni}$). Section 3.2.7 asks the converse direction: is $\mathrm{TT_I}$ itself conservative over "pure" type theory with *no* extensional concepts? Let $\mathrm{TT_p}$ be the core calculus plus $\mathrm{IdUni}$ but *without* $\mathrm{Ext}$.

Two negative data points before the main surprise:

- $\mathrm{IdUni}$ itself is not conservative over $J$-only type theory in any reasonable sense: $x{:}\sigma, u,v{:}\Phi[x] \vdash_I \mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma x:\sigma.\Phi[x]}(\mathrm{pair}(x,u),\mathrm{pair}(x,v)) \to \mathrm{Id}_\Phi(u,v)\ \mathrm{true}$ holds with UIP but not with $J$ alone (this is Streicher's original independence observation, the seed of the groupoid counterexample developed fully in Chapter 5).
- Hofmann *conjectures* (3.2.23, left open) that $\mathrm{TT_I}$ (with $\mathrm{IdUni}$ but not $\mathrm{Ext}$) is conservative over $\mathrm{TT_p}$ for $\Sigma$-free types — i.e. the specifically function-extensionality-flavored content is where the risk concentrates, not UIP by itself, provided you stay away from $\Sigma$.

The genuinely surprising result, though, is that **quotient types are not conservative over $\mathrm{TT_p}$ at all** — even though quotients look, on the surface, like they have nothing to do with functions or $\Pi$-types. Given $F,G : \Pi x{:}\sigma.\Phi$ and a pointwise proof $H : \Pi x{:}\sigma.\mathrm{Id}_\Phi(Fx,Gx)$ derivable in $\mathrm{TT_p}$ alone, Hofmann shows $\mathrm{Id}_{\Pi x:\sigma.\Phi}(\lambda x.Fx,\lambda x.Gx)$ becomes provable using *only* [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|intensional quotient types]] (no $\mathrm{Ext}$ primitive at all). The construction: quotient the function space $\Pi x{:}\sigma.\Phi$ by the relation $\mathrm{ExtEq}[u,v] := \Pi x{:}\sigma.\mathrm{Id}_\Phi(ux,vx)$, then use the quotient's lifting operator (`plug ... in ... using ...`) to define an `Extract` function from the quotient back out to $\Pi x{:}\sigma.\Phi$ that behaves like the identity on equivalence classes — and because $\mathrm{Qax}(H)$ witnesses $[F]_{\mathrm{ExtEq}} = [G]_{\mathrm{ExtEq}}$ in the quotient, $\mathrm{Resp}$ pushes this through `Extract` to yield $\mathrm{Id}_{\Pi\sigma.\Phi}(F,G)\ \mathrm{true}$ directly. (Getting all the way to a genuine $\eta$-flavored conclusion about *arbitrary* terms of $\Pi$-type, not just $\lambda$-abstractions, would additionally need $\Pi$-$\eta$ — but the pointwise-equality-implies-equal-functions core of functional extensionality is already there, quotients alone, no axiom.)

This matters far beyond a curiosity: it's a sharp warning that **logical strength is not additive across "independent-looking" features**. If you're designing a trusted kernel with a menu of optional extensions (quotient types, UIP, univalence-flavored equalities, `Prop` proof irrelevance...), you cannot assume two features are safe to combine just because each is independently conservative over the base theory and they look unrelated on paper — you have to check the *combination*. Quotients "for free" hand you a fragment of functional extensionality that you didn't ask for and that has its own non-canonicity cost (recall $\mathrm{Qax}$, like $\mathrm{Ext}$, introduces non-canonical elements unless a syntactic model gives it computational content — exactly the problem Chapter 5's setoid model exists to solve).

**Lean grounding.** This is precisely the caution behind why `Quot`/`Quotient` in Lean's kernel is a *primitive*, carefully restricted eliminator (`Quot.lift`, with `Quot.sound : a ~ b → Quot.mk r a = Quot.mk r b` as the one axiom-like ingredient) rather than something derived — and why Lean's `funext` (function extensionality) is proved *from* `Quot.sound` applied to the pointwise-equality relation on functions, in exactly the shape of the derivation above, rather than being an independent primitive. If you look at Lean 4's core library, `funext` genuinely is derived via a quotient of the function type by pointwise equality — this section of Hofmann's thesis is the theoretical explanation for why that derivation exists and works at all.

## Where this leads

This chapter is the fulcrum the rest of the thesis pivots on. Chapters 4–6 all take the same shape: *given* that $\mathrm{TT_I}+\mathrm{Ext}+\mathrm{IdUni}$ loses nothing (Cons-Ty) but genuinely needs those two concepts made computationally well-behaved (because axiomatizing them directly wrecks N-canonicity, as shown in §3.1.3), build syntactic categorical models — [[The-Deliverables-Model|the deliverables model]] (Ch. 4, subset types and proof irrelevance), and the setoid/groupoid/dependent-setoid models (Ch. 5, functional extensionality and quotient types) — that interpret the extensional concepts *inside* pure $\mathrm{TT_I}$, restoring canonicity while keeping the conservativity guarantee this chapter proved. The groupoid model's independence result for UIP (§5.2.3) is the payoff of the throwaway remark here in §3.1.2.2 about pattern matching defining $\mathrm{IdUni}$ "for free" — that's later turned into a proof that pattern matching for dependent types is itself a non-conservative extension. And Chapter 6's applications repeatedly lean on Cons-Ty as a *proof technique*: formalize a result in the more convenient $\mathrm{TT_E}$, invoke Theorem 3.2.5, and get an intensional witness for free rather than fighting nested $\mathrm{Subst}$-terms directly (the $\Sigma$-co-continuity result in §6.6 is the chapter's explicit poster child for this).

For the standing compiler/elaborator project, treat this chapter as settling, once and for all, the design question "should the trusted kernel ever reflect a propositional equality proof into definitional equality?" — the answer is no, on pain of losing decidable typechecking outright (§3.2.2), and the conservativity theorem (§3.2.5) is precisely the license to *not* worry that this refusal costs you expressive power, so long as functional extensionality and UIP are available in some form (as axioms first, then — following Chapters 4–5's method — as derived facts about a semantic model, restoring a fully computational, canonicity-respecting kernel). The non-conservativity of quotient types (§3.2.7) is the standing reminder to re-run a conservativity/canonicity check every time a new primitive (quotients, refinement predicates, a decidable-equality typeclass) gets added to that kernel, rather than assuming independence.
