# Extensional Concepts in Intensional Type Theory — Guidelines

## Header

**Title:** Extensional Concepts in Intensional Type Theory
**Author(s):** Martin Hofmann
**Publication:** PhD thesis, Laboratory for Foundations of Computer Science, University of Edinburgh, 1995

**Brief Summary:**
This thesis reconciles two rival treatments of equality in dependent type theory: extensional type theory, whose identity of propositions and definitional equality collapse into one undecidable notion, and intensional type theory, which is decidable but too weak for formalizing ordinary mathematics and certified programs. Hofmann's method is to interpret the missing "extensional concepts" — functional extensionality, uniqueness of identity proofs, proof irrelevance, subset types, propositional extensionality, and quotient types — as syntactic categorical models built entirely within pure intensional type theory, so that using them amounts to unfolding longer intensional derivations rather than adopting new axioms. The thesis develops this through a sequence of models (the deliverables model, the setoid model, the groupoid model, and a dependent setoid model), proves a key conservativity theorem justifying the approach, and settles the independence of uniqueness of identity proofs — showing pattern matching for dependent types is a non-conservative extension.

**Intent of the Author:**
Hofmann wants to show that adopting intensional type theory, with its decidable type-checking, need not be a genuine loss of expressive power for formalized mathematics and program verification — because the extensional concepts practitioners actually need can be recovered as definable "macros" inside intensional type theory via categorical model constructions, rather than requiring the undecidable extensional identity type.

## Chapter Summaries

### Chapter 1: Introduction (pp. 1–16)

**Summary:** Sets out the thesis's central tension — that intensional type theory's identity type is decidable and well-behaved but too weak for formalizing mathematics, while extensional type theory is strong enough but undecidable — and previews the method of interpreting "extensional concepts" back into intensional type theory via syntactic categorical models.

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**
- **1.1 Definitional and propositional equality** — definitional equality (equality induced by computation/reduction, expressed by the judgement $\Gamma \vdash M = N : \sigma$, pervasive under all contexts $P$) versus propositional equality (equality requiring a proof, expressed via Martin-Löf's identity type $\mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,N)$ with constructor $\mathrm{Refl}_\sigma(M) : \mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,M)$, or via Leibniz's principle: a proof that every property of $M$ holds of $N$); the equality reflection rule of extensional type theory, $\dfrac{\Gamma \vdash P : \mathrm{Id}_\sigma(M,N)}{\Gamma \vdash M = N : \sigma}$, which collapses the two notions and makes both definitional equality and type-checking undecidable; N-canonicity as the property that every closed natural-number term reduces to a numeral. : [[Definitional-Versus-Propositional-Equality|Link]]
- **1.2 Extensional concepts** — six extensional concepts studied in the thesis: functional extensionality (pointwise-equal functions are propositionally equal), uniqueness of identity (any two proofs of the same equality are equal), proof-irrelevance (any two proofs of the same proposition are equal), subset types (forming a type of elements satisfying a predicate), propositional extensionality (mutually implying propositions are equal), and quotient types (freely redefining propositional equality on a type); the type of propositions $\mathrm{Prop}$ and its proof type $\mathrm{Prf}(P)$ as the framework distinguishing propositions from arbitrary types; the observation that naively adding an extensionality axiom (e.g. $\mathrm{Ext}$) as a constant destroys N-canonicity because the axiom has no reduction behavior. : [[Applications-Of-Extensional-Concepts|Link1]], [[The-Method-Of-Syntactic-Models|Link2]], [[The-Setoid-Model|Link3]], [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link4]]
- **1.3 Method** — the general strategy of interpreting a type theory with extensional concepts *inside* pure (extensional-concept-free) type theory, so two objects are deemed definitionally equal exactly when their interpretations are; the *setoid interpretation* (each type becomes a type with an internal equivalence relation, inspired by Bishop's sets), giving functional extensionality and quotient types; the *deliverables interpretation* (each type becomes a type with a predicate, terms become functions with respect-proofs), giving subset types and proof-irrelevance; §1.3.1 motivates inserting an intermediate layer of abstract *categorical models* (analogous to using cartesian closed categories to interpret simply typed $\lambda$-calculus) so soundness needs verifying only once, against the abstract model, rather than per translation — here the abstract model is *syntactic categories with attributes*, an equational presentation admitting mechanized verification by term rewriting (as done in the Lego proof checker); §1.3.2 explains why the models sought must be *syntactic* (objects are syntactical, equality induced by definitional equality of the underlying theory), so that extensional concepts become "macros" for longer intensional derivations, plus the non-syntactic *groupoid model* as a further refinement.
- **1.4 Applications** — functional extensionality for reasoning about higher-order functions and coinductive types via function encodings; quotient types for constructive algebra and bisimulation; subset types for modular specification; §1.4.1 discusses machine-assisted theorem proving, contrasting axiomatically adding extensional concepts to an existing proof assistant (simple but breaks canonicity) versus modifying the proof assistant itself to compute syntactic-model interpretations internally (restores canonicity but requires substantial engineering).
- **1.5 Overview** — a roadmap: Chapter 2 gives syntax and abstract categorical semantics; Chapter 3 compares extensional and intensional type theory and proves a conservativity result; Chapter 4 gives the deliverables model (subset types, proof-irrelevance); Chapter 5, the thesis's core, gives three models (setoid, groupoid, dependent setoid) for extensionality and quotients; Chapter 6 works applications; Chapter 7 concludes.

**Key Questions:**
1. Why does identifying propositional equality with definitional equality via the equality reflection rule make *type-checking itself* undecidable, not just equality-checking?
2. In what precise sense are extensional concepts, once given a syntactic-model interpretation, "macros" for longer derivations in plain intensional type theory rather than genuinely new logical content?
3. Why is N-canonicity singled out as the crucial property to preserve when adding extensional concepts, and why does naively axiomatizing an extensional concept (e.g. functional extensionality as a bare constant) necessarily destroy it?

### Chapter 2: Syntax and semantics of dependent types (pp. 17–76)

**Summary:** Fixes a concrete syntax for a core dependently typed calculus (Martin-Löf type theory with $\Pi$-types, natural numbers, and identity types, later extended with $\Sigma$-types, the Calculus of Constructions, universes, and quotient types), then develops the categorical notion of model — *syntactic categories with attributes* — used throughout the thesis, together with a partial interpretation function proved sound with respect to the syntax. : [[Categorical-Semantics-Of-Dependent-Type-Theory|Link]]

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**
- **2.1 Syntax for a core calculus**
  - **2.1.1 Raw syntax** — pre-contexts, pre-types, and pre-terms defined by a grammar (Fig. 2-1) with explicit type annotations on abstraction/application; pre-constructions (the union of the three) as syntax not yet required to type-check; variables identified up to renaming rather than using de Bruijn indices, for readability.
  - **2.1.2 Judgements** — the six judgement forms: context well-formedness $\vdash \Gamma$, type formation $\Gamma \vdash \sigma$, term typing $\Gamma \vdash M : \sigma$, and the three definitional-equality judgements on contexts, types, and terms; type/term formation rules for $\Pi$ (introduction/elimination via abstraction/application), $\mathbb{N}$ (zero, successor, and the induction/elimination combinator $R_\mathbb{N}$), and $\mathrm{Id}$ (reflexivity introduction $\mathrm{Refl}$, and the Leibniz-style elimination rule $J$, studied further in Chapter 3); definitional equality as the least congruence closed under reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, and $\beta$/computation rules ($\Pi$-Beta, Nat-Comp-Zero/Suc, Id-Comp), contrasted with propositional equality's essential need for a proof term.
  - **2.1.3 Notation** — abbreviating derivations by omitting a shared ambient context; $\equiv$ for syntactic identity up to $\alpha$-conversion.
  - **2.1.4 Derived rules and meta-theoretic properties** — Weakening, Substitution, strong normalization of the rewrite system induced by orienting the computation rules (giving a decision procedure for definitional equality), decidability of typing, unicity of typing and of type formation; N-canonicity (Def. 2.1.9): every closed natural-number term reduces to an iterated successor of zero — shown to hold for the core calculus but destroyed by naive extensional axioms (as previewed in Ch. 1).
- **2.2 High-level syntax** — introduces derived notation needed for the model constructions.
  - **2.2.1 Telescopes** — a telescope is a context $\Delta$ relative to an ambient context $\Gamma$ (a sequence of type declarations each depending on the previous), written $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$.
  - **2.2.2 Elements of telescopes and context morphisms** — an element $f$ of a telescope $\Delta$ (an $n$-tuple of terms fitting $\Delta$'s dependent types); a context morphism / substitution $\Gamma \vdash f \Rightarrow \Delta$ when $\Delta$ doesn't depend on $\Gamma$; composition of context morphisms making contexts into a category; the observation that unicity of typing fails for telescope elements (the same tuple can be an element of differently-typed telescopes).
  - **2.2.3 Definitions and substitution** — the meta-level definition notation $\varphi[\Gamma : \Gamma] := M$ making $\varphi$ an abbreviation parametrized over $\Gamma$'s variables, used heavily for instantiating binding operators like $R_\mathbb{N}$. : [[Definitional-Versus-Propositional-Equality|Link]]
- **2.3 Further type formers** : [[The-Setoid-Model|Link]]
  - **2.3.1 Unit type** — the type $1$ with canonical inhabitant $\ast$ and an induction principle asserting $\ast$ is the only inhabitant up to propositional (not definitional) equality. : [[Categorical-Semantics-Of-Dependent-Type-Theory|Link1]], [[The-Setoid-Model|Link2]]
  - **2.3.2 $\Sigma$-types** — internalizing telescopes as dependent sums, with pairing and a Leibniz-style eliminator $R_\Sigma$; projections $.1$/$.2$ defined via $R_\Sigma$ satisfy the computation laws but pairing is not surjective, only "propositionally surjective." : [[Categorical-Semantics-Of-Dependent-Type-Theory|Link]]
  - **2.3.3 Function and cartesian product types** — $\sigma \to \tau$ and $\sigma \times \tau$ as special cases of $\Pi$/$\Sigma$ with a constant type family; the resulting core theory is named $TT$. : [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link]]
  - **2.3.4 The Calculus of Constructions** — Coquand–Huet's system adding an impredicative universe $\mathrm{Prop}$ closed under $\forall$-quantification, with $\mathrm{Prf}(S)$ the type of proofs of $S : \mathrm{Prop}$; standard logical connectives $\Rightarrow, \wedge, \vee, \mathrm{tt}, \mathrm{ff}, \neg, \exists$ and Leibniz equality $M =_L N$ encoded impredicatively as second-order formulas.
  - **2.3.5 Universes** — a universe $U$ with a decoding operator $\mathrm{El}$ and closure operators (e.g. $\hat\Pi$) mirroring type formers at the level of codes; universes can increase logical strength, e.g. yielding Peano's fourth axiom ($0 \neq \mathrm{Suc}(0)$) when a universe containing an empty type is present.
  - **2.3.6 Quotient types** — types permitting redefinition of propositional equality; the chapter defers their syntactic rules to Chapter 5, noting only that intensional quotient types necessarily introduce non-canonical identity-type elements, breaking N-canonicity unless definitional equality itself is redefined via a syntactic model. : [[Intensional-And-Extensional-Type-Theory|Link1]], [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link2]], [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link3]]
- **2.4 Abstract semantics of type theory** : [[Categorical-Semantics-Of-Dependent-Type-Theory|Link]]
  - **2.4.1 Syntactic categories with attributes** — the thesis's central model notion (a variant of Cartmell's categories with attributes/Pitts's "type categories," avoiding conditional equations by making sections a primitive sort, following Curien): a category $\mathcal{C}$ of contexts with terminal object; a functor $\mathrm{Fam} : \mathcal{C}^{op} \to \mathbf{Set}$ assigning to each context its set of families/types, with substitution written $\sigma\{f\}$; for each family $\sigma$ over $\Gamma$, a comprehension context $\Gamma.\sigma$ and display map/projection $p(\sigma) : \Gamma.\sigma \to \Gamma$; the weakening morphism $q(f,\sigma)$; a set $\mathrm{Sect}(\sigma)$ of sections (terms) with the characterizing equation $p(\sigma)\circ \ulcorner M \urcorner = \mathrm{id}$; the $\mathrm{Hd}$ operator recovering a section from a morphism into a comprehension; example models: the term model (Ex. 2.4.2, later shown initial), families over an arbitrary small category (Ex. 2.4.3), the set-theoretic model via small sets (Ex. 2.4.4), and families of $\omega$-sets via realizability (Ex. 2.4.5); Prop. 2.4.10 characterizes syntactic categories with attributes equivalently via pullback squares and a bijection between sections and pullback-induced right inverses, connecting to Cartmell's original (conditional-equation) notion (Remark 2.4.11); semantic variables $v_\sigma$ and weakening notation ($\sigma^+$, $M^+$, $f^+$). : [[Categorical-Semantics-Of-Dependent-Type-Theory|Link]]
  - **2.4.2 Type constructors** — semantic definitions of each type former as data plus substitution-stability equations, always designed so the term model is an instance (ruling out purely universal-property definitions): dependent products (§2.4.2.1, with an alternative "evaluation morphism" characterization in Prop. 2.4.17); unit types (§2.4.2.2); natural numbers (§2.4.2.3, related to the classical parametrized natural numbers object in a cartesian category); identity types (§2.4.2.4, with a simplified pullback-square characterization when $\mathrm{Re}_\sigma$ is an isomorphism, Prop. 2.4.25); $\Sigma$-types (§2.4.2.5, with the "extensional" special case when pairing is itself an isomorphism); universes (§2.4.2.6–8): full submodel (a subfunctor of $\mathrm{Fam}$), closure under a type former, closure under impredicative quantification, generic family $(U, \mathrm{El})$ classifying exactly the full submodel, and — because many natural models (set-theoretic, $\omega$-set) fail the strict generic-family definition — the weaker notion of *loose model* together with a canonical construction (Prop. 2.4.38) turning any loose model into a strict one. : [[Categorical-Semantics-Of-Dependent-Type-Theory|Link]]
- **2.5 Interpreting the syntax** : [[Categorical-Semantics-Of-Dependent-Type-Theory|Link]]
  - **2.5.1 Partial interpretation** — the semantic function $[\![-]\!]$ mapping pre-contexts/pre-types/pre-terms to objects/families/sections of a syntactic category with attributes equipped with $\Pi$, $\mathbb{N}$, and $\mathrm{Id}$, defined by structural clauses that are undefined whenever a subexpression or "type-checking" side-condition fails (following Streicher's method of interpreting *pre*-constructions first, then proving totality on well-formed ones, avoiding derivation-dependence issues). : [[Categorical-Semantics-Of-Dependent-Type-Theory|Link]]
  - **2.5.2 Soundness of the interpretation** — semantic telescopes (Def. 2.5.1) as the model-side analog of syntactic telescopes, shown themselves to form a syntactic category with attributes (Prop. 2.5.2); a Weakening Lemma (2.5.3) and an (implied) Substitution Lemma relating syntactic and semantic weakening/substitution, proved by induction on a notion of syntactic "weight"; the main Soundness Theorem (2.5.6): well-formed contexts/types/terms/morphisms and their definitional equalities have well-defined, matching semantic interpretations; a "trivial" Completeness Theorem (2.5.7) via the term model, showing the term model is initial among syntactic categories with attributes supporting the relevant type formers. : [[Categorical-Semantics-Of-Dependent-Type-Theory|Link]]
- **2.6 Discussion and related work** — contrasts the chapter's logical-framework-free syntax (with explicit substitution as a *defined*, not primitive, operation) against more modern logical-framework presentations; situates syntactic categories with attributes among Cartmell's categories with attributes/contextual categories, Streicher's categorical semantics of the Calculus of Constructions, and non-split (up-to-isomorphism) alternatives such as locally cartesian closed categories, fibrational models, and display map categories, noting that the non-split approaches still lack a fully worked-out syntax interpretation, especially for universes.

**Key Questions:**
1. Why does the author introduce the intermediate abstraction of *syntactic categories with attributes* rather than interpreting the syntax directly into concrete models like the term model or the set-theoretic model?
2. What is the difference between the "strict" generic-family definition of a semantic universe and a *loose model*, and why do natural models like the set-theoretic model satisfy only the looser notion?
3. Why must the semantic type formers (e.g. dependent products, identity types) be defined so that all data are explicitly *stable under substitution*, rather than characterized purely by universal properties as is common elsewhere in the literature?

### Chapter 3: Syntactic properties of propositional equality (pp. 77–121)

**Summary:** This chapter establishes the two main technical justifications for preferring intensional type theory (TTI) with added extensional concepts over extensional type theory (TTE): TTE's type-checking is undecidable, while TTE is nonetheless conservative over TTI (with functional extensionality and uniqueness of identity) as far as type inhabitation is concerned, so nothing is lost by staying intensional. : [[Definitional-Versus-Propositional-Equality|Link]]

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**
- **3.1 Intensional type theory** — the elimination operator $J$ for the identity type $\mathrm{Id}$; the derived operator $\mathrm{Subst}_{\Gamma,\Phi}(M,M',P,N)$ ("Leibniz substitution") for replacing a term by a propositionally equal one in a dependent type, together with its computation rule $\mathrm{Subst}(M,M,\mathrm{Refl}(M),N)=N$; derived $\mathrm{Sym}$/$\mathrm{Trans}$/$\mathrm{Resp}$ combinators built from $\mathrm{Subst}$. : [[Intensional-And-Extensional-Type-Theory|Link]]
  - **3.1.1 Substitution** — the alternative elimination operator $J'$ requiring the motive only at reflexivity, definable from $J$ via function types.
  - **3.1.2 Uniqueness of identity** — uniqueness of identity (UIP): for any $P,Q:\mathrm{Id}_A(M,N)$, the type $\mathrm{Id}_{\mathrm{Id}_A(M,N)}(P,Q)$ is inhabited; the constants $\mathrm{IdUni}$ added to achieve this when one proof is canonical (by reflexivity), with the general case then derivable via $J$; uniqueness of identity is *definable* (without axioms) at the unit type, the empty type, $\mathrm{Id}$-types, $\mathbb{N}$, and $\Sigma$-types, given a universe, but not at universes themselves; the connection between $\mathrm{IdUni}$ and Coquand's pattern-matching for dependent types. : [[Independence-Of-Uniqueness-Of-Identity-Proofs|Link1]], [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link2]]
  - **3.1.3 Functional extensionality** — functional extensionality is not derivable in intensional type theory (by a strong-normalization argument); the added constant $\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma,\Phi}(U,V,P) : \mathrm{Id}_{\Pi x:\Gamma.\Phi}(U,V)$ (Turner's solution) and its serious drawback of introducing non-canonical elements of every type (e.g. a term of type $\mathbb{N}$ in the empty context that is not definitionally a numeral, though it is propositionally equal to one); Turner's equation for reducing $\mathrm{Ext}$ applied to reflexivity proofs back to reflexivity; using $\mathrm{Ext}$ together with UIP to derive uniqueness of identity at $\Pi$-types. : [[Applications-Of-Extensional-Concepts|Link1]], [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link2]]
- **3.2 Extensional type theory** — the two extra rules defining TTE: the equality reflection rule ($\mathrm{Id\text{-}DefEq}$, from $P:\mathrm{Id}_A(M,N)$ conclude $M=N:A$) and $\mathrm{Id\text{-}Uni}$ (any proof of $\mathrm{Id}_A(M,N)$ equals $\mathrm{Refl}(M)$), plus an $\eta$-rule for $\Pi$-types; in TTE, $J$, $\mathrm{IdUni}$, and $\mathrm{Ext}$ all become definable/trivial (instances of reflexivity), so TTE needs no separate identity-elimination machinery. : [[Intensional-And-Extensional-Type-Theory|Link]]
  - **3.2.1 Comparison with Troelstra's presentation** — Troelstra and van Dalen's "intensional" $\mathrm{ML_0^i}$ is actually closer to the author's extensional theory (with syntactic-identity type equality), while their "extensional" $\mathrm{ML_0}$ matches TTE in this thesis's sense.
  - **3.2.2 Undecidability of extensional type theory** — Theorem: definitional equality, typechecking, and typehood in TTE are all equivalent to one another and undecidable, proved by encoding a self-halting-Turing-machine predicate as a numeral-wise representable function $f(e,t)$ and deriving a contradiction from an assumed decision procedure. : [[Intensional-And-Extensional-Type-Theory|Link]]
  - **3.2.3 Interpreting extensional type theory in intensional type theory** — the "stripping map" $|\cdot|$ erasing all TTI-specific term formers ($\mathrm{Subst}$, $\mathrm{IdUni}$, $\mathrm{Ext}$) to yield TTE terms, with the soundness property that every TTI derivation strips to a valid TTE derivation. : [[Intensional-And-Extensional-Type-Theory|Link]]
  - **3.2.4 An extension of TTI for which the interpretation in TTE is surjective** — the auxiliary term former $\triangleleft_A(M,N,P)$ (definitionally equal to $N$ but stripping to $M$), used to show the map $|\cdot|$ from the extended theory $\mathrm{TT}^{\triangleleft}$ onto TTE is surjective (property **Inv**), i.e. every TTE derivation lifts to some $\mathrm{TT}^{\triangleleft}$ derivation with matching stripping.
  - **3.2.5 Conservativity of TTE over TTI** — the main theorem (**Cons-Ty**): if $\Gamma$ is a valid TTI context and $|\Gamma|\vdash_E P:|A|$ for some $P$, then there exists $P'$ with $\Gamma\vdash_I P':A$; proved by quotienting the term model of TTI by propositional equality (via structurally-defined "propositional isomorphisms" $\mathrm{co}$/$\mathrm{ty}$ between types/contexts with the same stripping) to build a model $\mathcal{Q}$ of TTE, then composing interpretations to recover a TTI witness. : [[Intensional-And-Extensional-Type-Theory|Link]]
  - **3.2.6 Discussion and extensions** — the conservativity proof's non-constructive character (it uses choice and gives no effective algorithm, though one exists trivially by search); extension of the conservativity result to Nuprl-style extensional quotient types (§3.2.6.1) via analogous intensional quotient-type rules with an explicit witness term $\mathrm{Qax}$; extension to universes closed under $\Sigma$ and $\mathbb{N}$ (§3.2.6.2), where TTE with universes admits non-normalizing (but well-typed) terms. : [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link1]], [[Applications-Of-Extensional-Concepts|Link2]]
  - **3.2.7 Conservativity of quotient types and functional extensionality** — a converse question: whether TTI is conservative over pure type theory (no extensional concepts); Conjecture that this holds for $\Sigma$-free types; the surprising fact that intensional quotient types are *not* conservative over pure type theory, since a weak form of functional extensionality is derivable from them via a quotient by pointwise equality. : [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link1]], [[Applications-Of-Extensional-Concepts|Link2]], [[Intensional-And-Extensional-Type-Theory|Link3]]
- **3.3 Related work** — situates the results against Streicher's original identification of UIP and the $K$-axiom, Luo's Extended Calculus of Constructions work on equality reflection, Gabbay and de Queiroz's labelled deductive systems, and Mendler's categorical account of extensional quotient types as coequalizers.

**Key Questions:**
1. Why is definitional equality (and hence typechecking) in extensional type theory undecidable, and what role does the equality reflection rule $\mathrm{Id\text{-}DefEq}$ play in causing this, given that it looks like a harmless simplification?
2. What precisely does it mean for TTE to be "conservative" over TTI, and why does this justify treating intensional type theory (extended with $\mathrm{Ext}$ and $\mathrm{IdUni}$) as no less expressive than extensional type theory for the purpose of inhabiting types?
3. Why is it surprising that intensional quotient types are *not* conservative over plain intensional type theory, even though quotient types seem unrelated to functions or $\Pi$-types?

### Chapter 4: Proof irrelevance and subset types (pp. 123–158)

**Summary:** This chapter builds the "deliverables" categorical model $\mathcal{D}$, in which a specification is a type paired with a Prop-valued predicate, showing it supports proof irrelevance and subset types while reconciling two competing styles of certified program development — mixing specifications and code via refinement types, versus keeping them strictly separate as "deliverables." : [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link1]], [[The-Deliverables-Model|Link2]]

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**
- **4.1 The refinement approach** — types like $\mathrm{Even} := \Sigma n{:}\mathbb{N}.\exists m{:}\mathbb{N}.n =_L 2m$ pairing a value with a correctness proof; two problems with this style: the projection to the underlying type need not be injective, and a function out of a refined type may (syntactically) depend on the proof component even though, by strong normalization, computations of inductive type (e.g. $\mathbb{N}$) provably cannot actually depend on a proof-typed free variable (Proposition 4.1.1).
- **4.2 The deliverables approach** — a *deliverable* (McKinna's approach): a pair $(f_1,f_2)$ where $f_1$ is a genuine algorithm and $f_2$ is a separate proof that $f_1$ respects the specification's predicates; the drawback of heavy bookkeeping (composing function and proof parts separately) that this chapter's model construction aims to remove by organizing deliverables into a full categorical model of the Calculus of Constructions. : [[The-Deliverables-Model|Link1]], [[The-Method-Of-Syntactic-Models|Link2]]
- **4.3 The deliverables model** — the plan: contexts/families/sections in the model $\mathcal{D}$ are pairs of an ordinary syntactic ingredient (the "$\mathrm{set}$" component) and a proof-respecting ingredient (the "$\mathrm{pred}$" component). : [[The-Deliverables-Model|Link]]
  - **4.3.1 Contexts** — a *context of specifications* $(\Gamma,\Phi)$: an ordinary context $\Gamma$ plus a *propositional telescope* $\Phi$ (types all of the form $\mathrm{Prf}(M)$) over it; a *deliverable* morphism between two such contexts is a pair $(f,g)$ of an ordinary context morphism and a proof that it respects the target's predicates.
  - **4.3.2 Families of specifications** — a family over $\Gamma$ as a pair $(\mathrm{set},\mathrm{pred})$; comprehension $\Gamma\cdot\Delta$, substitution, and the generic projection $p(\Delta)$/pairing morphism $q(f,\Delta)$ defined component-wise, mirroring the ordinary term model in the $\mathrm{set}$-component while carrying proof obligations in the $\mathrm{pred}$-component.
  - **4.3.3 Sections of specifications (deliverables)** — a *section* (deliverable) as a pair $(M^{\mathrm{fun}},M^{\mathrm{resp}})$: an algorithm plus a proof it respects the predicate; Proposition 4.3.1 that specifications, families, and sections form a syntactic category with attributes — this structure is called $\mathcal{D}$.
- **4.4 Model checking with Lego** — using the Lego proof assistant to mechanically verify the categorical laws of $\mathcal{D}$, encoding record types and structural components via $\Sigma$-types since Lego lacks native contexts/telescopes; illustrated by machine-checked associativity and substitution-coherence proofs (§4.4.1 Records in Lego, §4.4.2 Deliverables in Lego). : [[The-Method-Of-Syntactic-Models|Link]]
- **4.5 Type formers in the model $\mathcal{D}$** — showing $\mathcal{D}$ supports the standard type formers. : [[The-Deliverables-Model|Link]]
  - **4.5.1 Dependent products** — the $\mathrm{set}$-component of a $\Pi$-type in $\mathcal{D}$ is the ordinary function space; its $\mathrm{pred}$-component expresses that a function sends predicate-respecting arguments to predicate-respecting results. : [[Categorical-Semantics-Of-Dependent-Type-Theory|Link]]
  - **4.5.2 Dependent sums** — analogous construction for $\Sigma$-types using the $\Sigma$-elimination operator $R_\Sigma$ to define the predicate on non-canonical elements.
  - **4.5.3 Natural numbers** — $(\mathbb{N}_\Gamma)^{\mathrm{pred}}$ is trivially true ($\mathrm{tt}$); Proposition 4.5.3 (**N-canonicity**): every section of $\mathbb{N}$ over the empty context is a numeral.
  - **4.5.4 The type of propositions** — the central construction: the family $\mathrm{Prop}$ with $\mathrm{set}$-component the syntactic $\mathrm{Prop}$ and trivial predicate, and the generic proof-type family $\mathrm{Prf}$ whose $\mathrm{set}$-component is always the (extensional) unit type $1_E$ but whose predicate *is* the proposition itself — this is what forces every proof of a given proposition to have the same $\mathrm{fun}$-component; universal quantification $\forall_\Gamma(s)$ and the evaluation morphism defined accordingly; Proposition 4.5.5 (**Consistency**): the family corresponding to False has no sections. §4.5.4.1 shows how to eliminate the need for an extensional unit type by instead allowing "propositional families" with no $\mathrm{set}$-component at all. : [[The-Setoid-Model|Link1]], [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link2]]
  - **4.5.5 Proof irrelevance** — a semantic Leibniz equality $\mathrm{LEq}$ defined via impredicative quantification; Proposition 4.5.7: any two sections (proofs) $M,N$ of a proposition $A$ in $\mathcal{D}$ satisfy $\mathrm{LEq}(M,N)$, because both have $\mathrm{fun}$-component forced to $\star{:}1_E$ — this yields **proof irrelevance** as a derivable rule ($\mathrm{Pr\text{-}Ir}$) rather than an axiom, though the two proofs need not be *definitionally* equal since their $\mathrm{resp}$-components (not observed propositionally) may differ. : [[The-Deliverables-Model|Link1]], [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link2]]
  - **4.5.6 Universes** — a universe $\mathrm{Set}$ (with family-valued elements $\mathrm{El}$) definable in $\mathcal{D}$ distinct from $\mathrm{Prop}$, since $\mathrm{Prop}$ is used both for proof-irrelevant logic and impredicative quantification, echoing the two impredicative universes $\mathrm{Spec}$/$\mathrm{Prop}$ of Paulin-Mohring's thesis.
- **4.6 Subset types** — given $\mathrm{Pr\text{-}Ir}$, the subset type $\{x{:}A \mid P\}$ can be modeled with $\mathrm{set}$-component equal to $A$'s $\mathrm{set}$-component (not a $\Sigma$-type over $1_E$), with associated $\mathrm{fg\text{-}Form}$/$\mathrm{fg\text{-}Intro}$/$\mathrm{fg\text{-}Wit}$ (witness)/$\mathrm{fg\text{-}Cor}$ (correctness proof)/$\mathrm{fg\text{-}Beta}$ rules; the first projection out of a subset type is *provably injective* using $\mathrm{Pr\text{-}Ir}$. : [[Applications-Of-Extensional-Concepts|Link1]], [[The-Deliverables-Model|Link2]], [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link3]]
  - **4.6.1 Subset types without impredicativity** — the same construction carries over to a two-sorted core type theory (types vs. propositions) without impredicative $\mathrm{Prop}$. : [[The-Deliverables-Model|Link]]
  - **4.6.2 A non-standard rule for subset types** — a further refinement admitting "non-propositional families" (types with no $\mathrm{pred}$-component, i.e. trivially $\mathrm{tt}$), yielding the non-standard rule $\mathrm{fg\text{-}Elim\text{-}Nonprop}$: any function defined on a subset type $\{x{:}A \mid P\}$ into a non-propositional type extends to a function on the whole of $A$ — used for *internal program extraction* (§4.6.2.1, extracting the "underlying algorithm" $\hat f$ of a verified function $f$) and for modeling *modules/signatures* as subset types of $\mathrm{Prop}\!\to\!\mathrm{Prop}$-style kinds (§4.6.2.2, e.g. encoding a monad signature $\mathrm{MON}$). : [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link]]
- **4.7 Reinterpretation of the equality judgement** — proposes replacing type theory's syntactic equality rules with *semantically* defined equality (via the interpretation $[\![\cdot]\!]$ into $\mathcal{D}$), yielding a type theory $\mathcal{D}$ with only three equality rules (term/type/context) that remains decidable because equality in $\mathcal{D}$ and in the underlying Calculus of Constructions is decidable; this reframes awkward rules like $\mathrm{fg\text{-}Elim\text{-}Nonprop\text{-}Comp}$ as instances of semantic equality. : [[Definitional-Versus-Propositional-Equality|Link]]
- **4.8 Related work** — situates $\mathcal{D}$ against Martin-Löf's subset interpretation (which identifies morphisms with equal first components, making equality undecidable), Salvesen and Smith's subset types for stable formulae, and Paulin-Mohring's two-universe ($\mathrm{Spec}$/$\mathrm{Prop}$) model.

**Key Questions:**
1. Why does giving every proof-type family the extensional unit type $1_E$ as its $\mathrm{set}$-component automatically yield proof irrelevance, and why is this only a semantic (not necessarily definitional) equality between distinct proofs?
2. What concrete bookkeeping problem does the "deliverables" style solve compared to freely mixing specifications and code with $\Sigma$-types, and what is lost or gained by keeping the two approaches "unified" inside the model $\mathcal{D}$ rather than choosing one?
3. What does the non-standard rule for subset types ($\mathrm{fg\text{-}Elim\text{-}Nonprop}$) let you do that ordinary subset-type elimination cannot, and why does this require introducing a separate notion of "non-propositional family"?

### Chapter 5: Extensionality and Quotient Types (pp. 158–225)

**Summary:** This is the thesis's core chapter, constructing three successive models — the setoid model $S_0$, the groupoid model, and a dependent setoid model $S_1$ — that interpret types as types-with-equivalence-relations in order to add functional extensionality and quotient types to intensional type theory, showing what each model gains and what it sacrifices. : [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link]]

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**
- **5.1 The setoid model** — setoid (a type together with a $\mathrm{Prop}$-valued partial equivalence relation, "$\mathrm{rel}$"); target type theory (the Calculus of Constructions the model is built in) vs. source type theory (the internal language of the model, containing the extensional concepts and a "strong" Leibniz equality); the model $S_0$ in which type dependency arises only at the level of relations, not of the underlying types themselves — hence it validates the substitution operator for Leibniz equality "for free." : [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link1]], [[The-Groupoid-And-Dependent-Setoid-Models|Link2]], [[The-Setoid-Model|Link3]]
  - **5.1.1 Contexts of setoids** — a context of setoids as a pair $(\Gamma_{\mathrm{set}}, \Gamma_{\mathrm{rel}})$ satisfying symmetry (Sym) and transitivity (Trans), with reflexivity deliberately *not* assumed; morphisms of setoid contexts (context morphisms respecting $\mathrm{rel}$, rule Resp) forming a category $\mathbf{C}$ with terminal object; families of setoids ($\mathrm{Fam}(\Gamma)$) with the compatibility condition Comp; substitution, comprehension ($\Gamma \cdot \Delta$), and sections of a family, together forming the syntactic category with attributes that is $S_0$.
  - **5.1.2 Implementing the setoid model $S_0$ in Lego** — the "deliverables" encoding technique: pairing the actual semantic data with a separately-ignored proof that it satisfies the required (extensional) properties, since Lego cannot compare propositional proofs definitionally. : [[The-Groupoid-And-Dependent-Setoid-Models|Link1]], [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link2]]
  - **5.1.3 Type formers in the setoid model** — dependent product, $\Sigma$-types, natural numbers, and other inductive types interpreted essentially uniquely; $N$-canonicity (in the empty context, natural numbers have only canonical elements) preserved despite the added extensional concepts. : [[The-Setoid-Model|Link]]
  - **5.1.4 Propositions** — interpreting an impredicative universe of propositions via bi-implication as the equivalence on $\mathrm{Prop}$; proof irrelevance (Pr-Ir), propositional extensionality (Bi-Imp: bi-implicative propositions are Leibniz-equal), and functional extensionality (Ext) all derivable in $S_0$; the surprising fact that Leibniz equality in $S_0$ behaves like an intensional identity type (admits a $\mathrm{Subst}$ operator), which shows $S_0$ is conservative over extensional type theory ($\mathrm{TT}_E$); §5.1.4.2 discusses why the internal axiom of choice (IAC) generally fails in the source theory (a choice witness need not respect equivalence relations, and IAC + Ext + Bi-Imp implies excluded middle); §5.1.4.3 shows Church's Thesis similarly fails (and is even refutable) once functional extensionality is present, though a weaker existential form $CT_0$ survives.
  - **5.1.5 Quotient types** — the syntax of intensional quotient types (rules Q-Form, Q-Intro, Q-Elim/Q-Comp, Q-Ax, Q-Ind) formalizing forming a quotient $\Gamma/R$ from a type and (not necessarily an equivalence) relation, defining functions on it via representatives, and asserting that related representatives yield Leibniz-equal classes; §5.1.5.1 relates this to the earlier (Sect. 3.2.6.1) presentation of quotients. : [[Intensional-And-Extensional-Type-Theory|Link1]], [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link2]], [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link3]]
  - **5.1.6 Interpretation of quotient types in $S_0$** — the quotient relation $(\Gamma/R)_{\mathrm{rel}}$ modeled as the least *suitable* relation (the symmetric-transitive closure of $R$ compatible with the pre-existing relation on $\Gamma$), defined via a higher-order impredicative encoding; construction of equivalence classes, lifting of functions respecting $R$ to functions on the quotient, and the induction principle; §5.1.6.4 defines *effective* quotient types (where $[M]_R =_L [N]_R$ implies $R[M,N]$ when $R$ is already an equivalence relation) and shows all quotients in $S_0$ are effective, using rule Bi-Imp. : [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link]]
  - **5.1.7 A choice operator for quotient types** — the paradoxical-looking rule Q-Choice letting one recover a representative $\mathrm{choice}(M) : \Gamma$ from $M : \Gamma/R$, subject to a syntactic "non-quotiented context" side condition (Def. 5.1.12) ensuring soundness; §5.1.7.1 works through why this does not yield an inconsistency (the offending instantiation is ill-typed) and gives applications: recovering an implementation of a function between quotients, and extracting the first digit of a real number defined as a quotient (while still being unable to define non-constant functions out of the reals). : [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link1]], [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link2]]
  - **5.1.8 Type dependency and universes** — a proof that $S_0$ admits no genuine type dependency beyond that induced by propositions (no universes, no large eliminations) via a contradiction argument using an empty type; sketch of an extended setoid model admitting a universe restricted so that quotienting is allowed only for "small" types (those of the form $\mathrm{El}(M)$). : [[The-Setoid-Model|Link]]
- **5.2 The groupoid model** — motivation: to get real (non-proposition-induced) type dependency one must make the *type* component of a family depend on the base, which requires a reindexing operation using the proof of relatedness computationally, forcing that proof itself to live in a proper (proof-relevant) dependent type rather than a mere proposition; groupoid (a small category in which every morphism is an isomorphism, given by $X_{\mathrm{set}}$, $X_{\mathrm{rel}}$, and coherent $\mathrm{refl}$/$\mathrm{sym}$/$\mathrm{trans}$ operations satisfying groupoid equations); groupoids form a cartesian closed category; every type in intensional type theory induces a groupoid $G(\Gamma)$ whose morphisms are proofs of the identity type modulo propositional equality. : [[The-Groupoid-And-Dependent-Setoid-Models|Link]]
  - **5.2.1 Groupoids** — definitions of morphisms of groupoids (functors), the exponential (functor category) construction $X \Rightarrow Y$ via natural transformations, families of groupoids over a groupoid (a functor to the category of groupoids) with reindexing ($\mathrm{reindex}$) and its coherence conditions, comprehension via the Grothendieck construction.
  - **5.2.2 Interpretation of type formers** — dependent sum and dependent product of groupoids (with natural transformations as the relevant morphisms); §5.2.2.3 defines the identity groupoid $\mathrm{Id}(\sigma)$ as the discrete groupoid on the relation, making propositional and definitional equality on identity types coincide (illustrated by the homotopy-of-paths-in-a-topological-space intuition); Re, and the identity elimination operator $J$ interpreted via reindexing; §5.2.2.4 shows functional extensionality holds automatically; §5.2.2.5 interprets base types (0, 1, $N$) as discrete groupoids; §5.2.2.6 interprets universes using an inaccessible cardinal (the groupoid of all small groupoids), noting a non-trivial impredicative universe requires moving to a category like $\omega$-Sets; §5.2.2.7 sketches intensional quotient types when the quotienting relation is internally an equivalence relation. : [[The-Setoid-Model|Link1]], [[The-Method-Of-Syntactic-Models|Link2]]
  - **5.2.3 Uniqueness of identity** — Theorem 5.2.8: uniqueness of identity is *not* uniformly definable in the groupoid model (witnessed by $\Sigma x{:}U.\mathrm{El}(x)$ having an object, $\mathbb{Z}_2 \cdot \{*\}$, with two distinct automorphisms), giving the promised proof (foreshadowed in §3.1.2) that Martin-Löf's elimination rule $J$ alone cannot prove all equality proofs equal; Corollary 5.2.9: pattern matching is therefore a *non-conservative* extension of Martin-Löf type theory (since pattern matching can derive uniqueness of identity). : [[Independence-Of-Uniqueness-Of-Identity-Proofs|Link1]], [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link2]]
  - **5.2.4 Propositional equality as isomorphism** — a new, sound extension (rule Univ-Id) identifying propositional equality of universe codes with the type of isomorphisms $\mathrm{Iso}[X,Y]$ between their interpretations, with $\mathrm{Subst}$ along such a proof acting exactly as applying the isomorphism (Univ-Id-Eq); this deliberately violates uniqueness of identity yet is consistent, interpreted using the groupoid of all small sets with bijections as morphisms. : [[Independence-Of-Uniqueness-Of-Identity-Proofs|Link]]
- **5.3 A dependent setoid model** — model $S_1$: an attempt at a genuinely dependent setoid notion definable *inside* intensional type theory (unlike the set-theoretic groupoid model), obtained by dropping the requirement that context morphisms preserve symmetry/transitivity (since this cannot be guaranteed once function spaces are present) while still forcing preservation of reflexivity via a "trick"; the cost is that reflexivity is not preserved by reindexing, so several computation rules ($\beta$-Comp-style, Nat-Comp-Suc) hold only up to propositional, not definitional, equality — but $S_1$ does validate uniqueness of identity and functional extensionality, giving it the expressive power of extensional type theory $\mathrm{TT}_E$. : [[The-Groupoid-And-Dependent-Setoid-Models|Link]]
  - **5.3.1 Families of setoids** — context of setoids as a triple $(\Gamma_{\mathrm{set}}, \Gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}, \Gamma_{\mathrm{refl}})$ (a reflexive graph); families of setoids as 6-tuples ($\mathrm{set}$, $\mathrm{rel}$, $\mathrm{reindex}$, $\mathrm{sym}$, $\mathrm{trans}$, $\mathrm{ax}$) where reindexing requires elements to be "connected" ($\mathrm{conn}$) rather than merely related, since $\mathrm{rel}$ need not be symmetric; comprehension, substitution, and sections (§5.3.1.1–5.3.1.2), with sections shown to automatically preserve reflexivity — the key "trick" making $S_1$ work.
  - **5.3.2 Dependent product** — construction of $(\Sigma,\Pi)$-style function families over $S_1$, with the transitivity proof requiring $\mathrm{reindex}$ to find a common connected midpoint — showing why the more restrictive "connected" notion (rather than plain relatedness) is essential for defining $\Pi$-types. : [[Categorical-Semantics-Of-Dependent-Type-Theory|Link]]
  - **5.3.3 The identity type** — interpreting propositional equality directly as the setoid's own relation (endowed with a trivial, always-inhabited relation on proofs) so that uniqueness of identity holds and $J$ can be replaced by the simpler $\mathrm{Subst}$; the identity-elimination computation rule Leibniz-Comp holds only propositionally, not definitionally, because $\mathrm{reindex}$ along a reflexivity proof need not be the identity function. : [[Definitional-Versus-Propositional-Equality|Link]]
  - **5.3.4 Inductive types** — natural numbers modeled via the target theory's own identity type as the relation; the computation rule Nat-Comp-Suc again holds only propositionally; N-canonicity and consistency (Props. 5.3.10–5.3.11) still established; §5.3.4.1 notes the analogous difficulty for $\Sigma$-types, where only a *weak* elimination rule (no definitional second-projection equation) can be interpreted. : [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link]]
  - **5.3.5 Quotient types** — general intensional quotient types in $S_1$ are conjectured definable but too complicated to verify even with machine support; instead a squash type former ($\Box\Gamma$, quotienting by the everywhere-true relation) is constructed explicitly (Prop. 5.3.12), used to define an existential quantifier with a restricted elimination rule (target type must be subsingleton) validating unique choice; §5.3.5.1 notes a universe closed under $\Sigma$-types could not be identified in $S_1$. : [[Intensional-And-Extensional-Type-Theory|Link1]], [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link2]], [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link3]]
  - **5.4 Discussion and related work** — situates the setoid/PER approach against Gandy's and Feferman's classical extensionality-conservativity results, Bishop's constructive-analysis assemblies, Benabou's "empirical sets" and the Scott–Fourman/tripos-to-topos construction, and Jacobs's/Mendler's categorical treatments of quotient types; the key contrast drawn is functions-preserving-relations (used here, keeping propositionally-equal elements intensionally distinct and decidable) versus functional-relations-as-morphisms (used in topos-theoretic treatments, which gives unique choice for free but loses decidable semantic equality).

**Key Questions:**
1. Why does the setoid model $S_0$ fail to provide genuine dependent types (e.g. a family of types varying by primitive recursion over the natural numbers), and what specific structural feature of the groupoid model fixes this at the cost of no longer being definable inside intensional type theory?
2. In what precise sense does the groupoid model prove that Martin-Löf's elimination rule $J$ alone cannot derive uniqueness of identity, and why does this make pattern matching a non-conservative extension of intensional type theory?
3. Why must the choice operator for quotient types (Q-Choice) be restricted to "non-quotiented" contexts, and how does the book's own worked example ($\overline{1} =_L \overline{1'}$ in $\mathrm{Int}$) show what would go wrong — via ill-typedness rather than outright contradiction — if the restriction were dropped?
4. In the dependent setoid model $S_1$, why does dropping the requirement that context morphisms preserve symmetry and transitivity (while still preserving reflexivity) force certain definitional equalities, like Nat-Comp-Suc, to hold only propositionally — and why does the author still consider $S_1$ inferior to the groupoid model despite being syntactically definable?

### Chapter 6: Applications (pp. 226–259)

**Summary:** Works through six small case studies — Tarski's fixpoint theorem, streams, category theory, coproduct types, Bourbaki-style quotient constructions, and a co-continuity result — to demonstrate concretely why the extensional concepts (subset types, functional extensionality, quotient types, proof irrelevance) studied earlier in the thesis are useful, and how they get eliminated when translated into the setoid models.

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**
- **6.1 Tarski's fixpoint theorem** — a *complete lattice* $(T,\leq)$ is a poset in which every subset $P \subseteq T$ has a least upper bound $\bigvee P$; Tarski's theorem: the fixpoints of a monotone $f : T \to T$ form a complete lattice; the formalization (based on Pollack's, but using subset types and proof irrelevance from the deliverables model of Chapter 4) represents a poset as merely a type with a relation rather than a type-with-predicate, since deliverables already model ordinary types as pairs of a type and a $\mathrm{Prop}$-valued predicate; predicates $\mathrm{lower}$, $\mathrm{upper}$, $\mathrm{lub}$, $\mathrm{glb}$, $\mathrm{mon}$, $\mathrm{closed}$, $\mathrm{fix}$, $\mathrm{prefix}$ used to state and prove the theorem; the up-closure $\mathord{\uparrow}x := \{y : T \mid x \leq y\}$ of an element as a poset in its own right, requiring subset types to form. : [[Applications-Of-Extensional-Concepts|Link]]
  - **6.1.1 Discussion** — coercion functions between a subset type and its supertype are unavoidable overhead in this style of formalization (reported as "extremely cumbersome" in related work by B. Reus); the subset-type proof is nonetheless judged more faithful to the informal set-theoretic proof than Pollack's original Calculus-of-Constructions proof.
- **6.2 Streams in type theory** — $\mathrm{Str}(\sigma) := N \to \sigma$ (streams as functions from naturals); $\mathrm{Hd}$, $\mathrm{Tl}$; *bisimulation* as a relation $R$ on streams respecting head-equality and closed under tail; the *coinduction principle* (bisimilar streams are propositionally equal), proved using functional extensionality; *co-iteration* $\mathrm{CoIt}$ constructing a stream from an output map and a step map, satisfying its defining equations definitionally; *terminality* of $\mathrm{Str}(\sigma)$ up to propositional equality; *prefixing* $\mathrm{Pref}$; the domain-theoretic motivation via *dataflow networks* and *liveness* (the absence of a deadlocking/terminating stream, built into the type $\mathrm{Str}(\sigma)$ rather than proved separately); since no general fixpoint combinator exists for $\mathrm{Str}(\sigma)$, a *candidate fixpoint* construction $\mathrm{Fix}(F,M)$ together with a $\mathrm{Total}$ predicate (defined as a greatest fixpoint) used to prove, via coinduction, that $\mathrm{Fix}(F,M)$ is a genuine fixpoint of $F$ whenever $F$ is $\mathrm{Total}$. : [[Applications-Of-Extensional-Concepts|Link1]], [[Categorical-Semantics-Of-Dependent-Type-Theory|Link2]], [[Intensional-And-Extensional-Type-Theory|Link3]]
- **6.3 Category theory in type theory** — a *category* defined using propositional equality on both objects and morphisms (types $\mathrm{Ob}$, $\mathrm{Mor}[x,y]$, $\mathrm{id}$, $\mathrm{comp}$, and coherence laws $\mathrm{idL}$, $\mathrm{idR}$, $\mathrm{Ass}$ up to $\mathrm{Id}$); this definition is stable under functor/arrow/slice/opposite category constructions and yields $\mathrm{Full}(\Gamma,\Phi)$ (comparable to the category of small sets) for a universe; using $\mathrm{Subst}$ to transport morphisms along propositional equality of objects. : [[Applications-Of-Extensional-Concepts|Link]]
  - **6.3.1 Categories in $S_0$** — translating the definition into $S_0$: objects become a type with a partial equivalence relation, morphisms a *single* type independent of domain/codomain (precluding $\mathrm{Full}(U,\mathrm{El})$ in the base model, but not in its universe extension, distinguishing "small" categories with a genuine set of objects).
  - **6.3.2 Categories in $S_1$** — translating into $S_1$: morphisms are indexed by objects and a *reindexing* operator transports morphisms along object-equality proofs, recovering full stability under functor/arrow/slice categories at the cost of substantially more complex definitions.
  - **6.3.3 Discussion** — two justifications for a type-theoretic foundation of category theory: it exposes the constructive content of category-theoretic arguments hidden by set-theoretic quotienting/choice, and its consistency can be proved by elementary means, unlike Mac Lane-style universes in ZFC.
- **6.4 Encoding of the coproduct type** — the standard *coproduct type* interface ($\mathrm{inl}$, $\mathrm{inr}$, dependent eliminator with computation rules); the Troelstra–van Dalen encoding of $\sigma+\tau$ using the two-element type $N_2$ and its "ex falso"-style terms $\bot_\rho$, requiring functional extensionality and uniqueness of identity proofs to validate the encoding's computation rules only *propositionally*. : [[Applications-Of-Extensional-Concepts|Link]]
  - **6.4.1 Development in the setoid models** — the encoding transfers directly into $S_1$; in $S_0$ it requires an explicit case-by-case definition of $\bot_\rho$ by induction on $\rho$, exploiting proof irrelevance ($\mathrm{Prf}$) and illustrating concretely how functional extensionality gets "compiled away" into the reindexing structure of the model. : [[The-Groupoid-And-Dependent-Setoid-Models|Link1]], [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link2]], [[The-Setoid-Model|Link3]]
- **6.5 Some basic constructions with quotient types** — reconstructing, in $S_0$, universal-algebra-style quotient constructions from Bourbaki's set theory text. : [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link1]], [[The-Six-Extensional-Concepts|Link2]]
  - **6.5.1 Canonical factorisation of a function** — *surjection* and *injection* defined via $\Leftrightarrow$/first-order conditions; without the axiom of choice, a bijective function need not have an inverse (would require *unique choice*, $\mathrm{AC}^{!}$); factoring $f$ through the quotient $\sigma/R$ by $R[x,x'] := f[x] =_L f[x']$ gives an initial surjection–injection factorization; factoring through the image $\mathrm{Im}(f)$ gives a terminal one; the two factorizations coincide up to isomorphism only given $\mathrm{AC}^{!}$.
  - **6.5.2 Some categorical properties of $S_0$** — *regular epi*/*regular mono* defined via coequalizers/equalizers; the category $S_0/{=_L}$ (closed types, functions up to propositional equality) is *regular*, and unique choice is exactly the statement that every surjection is a regular epi (equivalently, that $S_0/{=_L}$ with functional-relation morphisms forms a *topos*).
  - **6.5.3 Subsets and quotients** — a bijective correspondence between the quotient of a subset $\sigma := \{x : \tau \mid P[x]\}$ by an induced relation and the "image" of $\sigma$ inside $\tau/R$, constructed using the dependent quotient-elimination rule $Q$-$I$-$Elim$. : [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link]]
  - **6.5.4 Saturated subsets** — a predicate $P$ is *saturated* w.r.t. $R$ if it is closed under $R$-related elements; saturated subsets of $\tau$ correspond bijectively to arbitrary subsets of $\tau/R$, using propositional extensionality and *effectiveness* of the quotient type. : [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link]]
  - **6.5.5 Iterated quotients** — $(\tau/R)/S \cong \tau/T$ where $T$ is the relation induced by composing $R$ and $S$; Bourbaki's notion of the "quotient of $T$ by $R$." : [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link]]
  - **6.5.6 Quotients and products** — $(\tau/R)\times(\upsilon/S) \cong (\tau\times\upsilon)/(R\times S)$, constructed via iterated lifting ($\mathrm{plug}$) and used as a template for defining binary functions on quotient types. : [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link]]
  - **6.5.7 Quotients and function spaces** — whether $\rho \to \tau/S \cong (\rho\to\tau)/({\to}S)$ is, unlike the previous constructions, *not* derivable from the quotient-type rules alone; it is shown equivalent to every class map being *powerful*, which in turn is equivalent to the internal axiom of choice ($\mathrm{IAC}$); since $S_0$ is not Boolean/does not validate $\mathrm{IAC}$ in general, this isomorphism cannot be proved and would have to be added as a further primitive. : [[Intensional-Quotient-Types|Link]]
- **6.6 $\Sigma$ is co-continuous—intensionally** — Mendler's result that "$\Sigma$-functors" $f : C/A \to C/B$ in a locally cartesian closed category preserve $\omega$-chain limits, transferred from extensional type theory (TTE) to intensional type theory (TTI) via the conservativity theorem (Thm. 3.2.5) rather than by a direct (much harder) intensional proof; a $\Sigma$-indexed family of $\omega$-chain limits is again a limit of the $\Sigma$-indexed family of chains, crucially using the *proof-relevant* nature of $\Sigma$ (a counterexample shows the analogous statement fails for propositional/existential $\exists$).
  - **6.6.1 Parametrised limits of $\omega$-chains** — informal set-theoretic statement and proof of Mendler's proposition, defining the limit $L$ as the set of compatible families.
  - **6.6.2 Development in TTE** — formalizing the same proof almost unchanged in extensional type theory, using $\mathrm{CompFam}$ (compatible families) as the limit.
  - **6.6.3 Development in TTI** — the conservativity theorem immediately transfers the TTE result to TTI, but a *direct* TTI proof is reported as extremely difficult to carry out in Lego, requiring reasoning about nested $\mathrm{Subst}$ terms; the chapter's conclusion is that conservativity, not brute-force intensional formalization, is the practical route to such results, motivating tactic support built on the conservativity theorem.

**Key Questions:**
1. In the Tarski fixpoint-theorem formalization, why does using the deliverables model (rather than raw intensional type theory) let posets be represented merely as "a type with a relation," and what specific difficulty (coercions between subsets and supersets) does this trade-off still leave unresolved?
2. Why does the isomorphism $\rho \to \tau/S \cong (\rho\to\tau)/({\to}S)$ fail to be derivable from the basic quotient-type rules, and what is the precise connection between this failure, the notion of a "powerful" surjection, and the internal axiom of choice?
3. Why is a direct proof of the $\Sigma$-co-continuity result difficult to carry out in intensional type theory, and what does the fact that conservativity (Thm. 3.2.5) yields the result "almost for free" suggest about the practical role of extensional syntactic models in this thesis's overall method?

### Chapter 7: Conclusions and Further Work (pp. 260–261)

**Summary:** A short closing chapter recapping the thesis's central move — justifying extensional concepts added to intensional type theory via syntactic models based on pure intensional type theory — and laying out several concrete directions for future work, mostly centered on implementing and better understanding the setoid models.

**Key Definitions & Concepts:**
- Implementing the syntactic models directly (beyond the existing variable-free Lego combinators of Sect. 4.4/5.1.2, which are of limited practical use) as a mostly engineering task.
- Implementing the *groupoid model* specifically, which (unlike the setoid models) is based on classical extensional set theory and is not directly implementable as given, though the fragment of it actually reached by the interpretation function is conjectured to admit an effective description.
- The idea (from Sect. 5.2.4) of interpreting propositional equality on types as isomorphism, absent uniqueness of identity proofs, as a promising but only lightly explored direction — including a sketch of an extended, coherent notion of category stable under functors and slices, requiring universes with propositional equality as isomorphism as a new primitive, which in turn would need a "proof-relevant" quotient type former to construct rather than posit.
- The open question of the expressiveness of the source type theories relative to the setoid models: whether every setoid in a model is isomorphic to one hit by the interpretation function, and whether every section of a definable family is itself definable — a completeness question the author regards as not fully answered and only partly useful even if resolved.
- The need for a fuller meta-theory of quotient types (e.g. the role of effectiveness, illustrated only partially by the Chapter 6 examples) and a more systematic study of which type formers (inductive types, universes) are interpretable in the models, especially $S_1$, and whether difficulties there are inherent to the approach or artifacts of the particular model definitions.

**Key Questions:**
1. Why does the author single out the groupoid model, rather than the setoid models $S_0$/$S_1$, as the harder and more theoretically interesting implementation challenge?
2. What is the relationship between the open "expressiveness" question (whether every setoid is isomorphic to a definable one) and the thesis's actual results — why does the author consider a positive answer to this question only partially useful?
3. What new primitive would be required to support the sketched extension of category theory based on interpreting propositional equality on types as isomorphism, and why can it not simply be constructed from the extensional concepts already studied in the thesis?
