# Interoperability between proof systems using the logical framework Dedukti — Guidelines

## Header

**Title:** Interoperability between proof systems using the logical framework Dedukti
**Author(s):** François Thiré
**Publication:** PhD thesis (Thèse de doctorat), Université Paris-Saclay, defended 3 December 2020 (NNT: 2020UPASG053). Advisors: Gilles Dowek and Stéphane Graham-Lengrand.

**Brief Summary:**
This thesis studies interoperability between proof assistants at the level of their type systems rather than their surface syntax. Part I develops **Cumulative Type Systems (CTS)** as a common meta-theoretic framework in which the logics behind Coq, Matita, Lean, Agda and the HOL family can all be presented as instances of one parameterized family, and it gives a theory of when a proof in one CTS can be re-expressed, sound and (partially) complete, in another via an embedding into the **$\lambda\Pi$-calculus modulo theory**. Part II turns this theory into working software: **Dedukti** (an implementation of $\lambda\Pi$-calculus modulo theory), **Dkmeta** (rewriting as a meta-programming language for proof transformations), and **Universo** (an SMT-backed tool that decides universe-level embeddings), which are combined to translate part of Matita's arithmetic library — culminating in a proof of Fermat's little theorem — into a constructive Higher-Order Logic (STT∀) and then re-export it to Coq, Lean, Matita, PVS and OpenTheory via the **Logipedia** website.

**Intent of the Author:**
Thiré sets out to explain, and partially automate, an empirical observation: libraries built on very different proof assistants tend to look strikingly similar, because the underlying logics are themselves similar. The thesis argues that CTS is the right common framework to make this precise, and aims to demonstrate — not just claim on paper — that proof-level interoperability is practically achievable by shipping a working translation pipeline for a real, nontrivial theorem.

## Chapter Summaries

### Chapter 1: Cumulative Type Systems (pp. 25–52)

**Summary:** Introduces the syntax and rewriting theory shared by every system in the thesis, then defines Cumulative Type Systems (CTS) — Pure Type Systems (PTS) extended with subtyping on sorts and product codomains — and surveys concrete CTS specifications, closing with their core meta-theoretic properties. : [[Cumulative-Type-Systems-(CTS)|Link]]

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**
- **1.1 Syntax** — term syntax $x \mid s \mid MN \mid \lambda x{:}A.M \mid (x{:}A)\to B$ (Definition 1.1.1); free variables; syntactic context (a term with a hole). : [[Dedukti-An-Implementation-of-Lambda-Pi-calculus-Modulo-Theory|Link]]
- **1.2 Rewriting** — rewriting relation stable by context, redex, Church-Rosser (CR)/confluence, normal form (NF), weak/strong normalization (WN/SN); the $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\eta$, $\delta$ (global definitions), $\zeta$ (local `let`) relations; $\beta\eta$ is *not* CR in general (Nederpelt's counterexample) but is CR for well-typed terms (Geuvers).
- **1.3 Cumulative Type Systems specification** — a CTS specification $\mathcal{C}=(S,A,R,C)$ (sorts, axioms, rules, cumulativity); top-sort; PTS as a CTS with $C=\emptyset$; finite/functional/injective/semi-full/full CTS; ordered specification, impredicative sort, predicative CTS; decidable CTS specification. : [[Cumulative-Type-Systems-(CTS)|Link]]
- **1.4 Typing** — the subtyping judgment $A \sqsubseteq_{\mathcal C} B$ generated by cumulativity (covariant on codomains only); product injectivity lemma; the two-judgment typing system ($\Gamma \vdash_{\mathcal C} t:A$ and $\Gamma \vdash_{\mathcal C} \mathrm{wf}$).
- **1.5 Programming with Pure Type Systems** — Simply Typed Lambda Calculus, System F, System F$\omega$, LF, the Calculus of Constructions, the $\lambda$-cube, $\lambda$HOL, System $U$/$U^-$, Agda's predicative cumulative hierarchy, and the concrete specifications for Lean, Coq and Matita. : [[Cumulative-Type-Systems-(CTS)|Link]]
- **1.6 Termination** — $\lambda U^-$ is not SN (Hurkens); the Calculus of Constructions is SN. : [[Cumulative-Type-Systems-(CTS)|Link]]
- **1.7 Meta-theory of Cumulative Type Systems** — well-sorted terms, weakening, the inversion lemmas (variable, sort, product, abstraction, application), the substitution lemma, subject reduction, type uniqueness for functional specifications, and transitivity of the CTS subtyping relation. : [[Cumulative-Type-Systems-(CTS)|Link]]

**Key Questions:**
1. What extra structure does a CTS specification add on top of a PTS specification, and why is subtyping restricted to be covariant on codomains only (not contravariant on domains)?
2. Why does $\beta\eta$ fail to be Church-Rosser in general, and what property of a term recovers confluence?
3. What distinguishes an impredicative sort from a predicative one, and why does this distinction matter for systems like the Calculus of Constructions?

### Chapter 2: Embeddings of CTS Specifications (pp. 53–76)

**Summary:** Formalizes what it means for one CTS to be embeddable in another (soundly re-derivable), develops the machinery — functionalization, injectivization, and the *free CTS* — needed to turn "is this judgment embeddable" into a solvable constraint problem, and gives an incomplete decision procedure together with a partial completeness result. : [[Bi-Directional-Type-Systems|Link1]], [[Cumulative-Type-Systems-(CTS)|Link2]], [[Dedukti-An-Implementation-of-Lambda-Pi-calculus-Modulo-Theory|Link3]], [[Interoperability-Between-Type-Systems|Link4]]

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**
- **2.1 Equivalences between CTS** — specification morphism (Definition 2.1.1) and its soundness theorem; sort erasure; judgment $\star$-embedding and judgment embedding; CTS embedding and CTS equivalence; the minimal specification. : [[Rewriting-as-a-Meta-Programming-Language-(Dkmeta)|Link1]], [[Well-Structured-Derivation-Trees|Link2]]
- **2.1.3–2.1.5 Weak embeddings** — inhabitation of a type, canonical inhabitant; weak judgment ($\star$-)embedding; weak CTS embedding and weak CTS equivalence.
- **2.2 Meta-theory of equivalences** — strict weak CTS embedding; preservation of termination across embeddings; CTS functionalization (Definition 2.2.2); CTS injectivization (Definition 2.2.3); top-sort-regular CTS; CTS with at most one top-sort. : [[Cumulative-Type-Systems-(CTS)|Link]]
- **2.3 Deciding judgment embeddings** — the free equivalence relation for conversion and for subtyping; the *free CTS* (Definition 2.3.3), which associates a specification to a derivation tree so that replacing every sort with a fresh variable stays type-checkable. : [[Universo-Deciding-Universe-Level-Embeddings|Link]]
- **2.4 Completeness** — a preorder (and strict preorder) on derivation trees used to argue partial completeness of the embedding procedure.
- **2.5 Future work.** : [[Philosophy-and-Future-of-Interoperability|Link1]], [[STT-A-Constructive-Higher-Order-Logic|Link2]], [[Well-Structured-Derivation-Trees|Link3]]

**Key Questions:**
1. What is the free CTS construction, and how does it turn "can judgment $J$ in $\mathcal C$ be embedded in $\mathcal C'$?" into a constraint-satisfaction problem?
2. Why is the judgment-embedding decision procedure only incomplete (rather than fully decidable), and what open conjectures would be needed to recover completeness?

### Chapter 3: Well-Structured Derivation Trees (pp. 77–100)

**Summary:** Introduces a notion of "well-structured" derivation tree — one where the has-type ordering is well-founded and compatible with $\beta$ — as a tool for attacking two hard open problems: expansion postponement, and the equivalence between an untyped ("syntactic") and a typed ("semantic") presentation of conversion/subtyping. : [[Well-Structured-Derivation-Trees|Link]]

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**
- **3.1 Well-structured derivation trees** — the subtree, has-type, and $\beta$ relations on derivation trees (Definition 3.1.1); well-structured derivation tree (Definition 3.1.2, the central definition of the chapter); well-structured judgments. : [[Well-Structured-Derivation-Trees|Link]]
- **3.2 Expansion postponement** — a typing system without expansions; the expansion-postponement (EP) property, conjectured to hold for every well-structured derivation tree. : [[Well-Structured-Derivation-Trees|Link]]
- **3.3 Semantic CTS** — a typing system with explicit subtyping (casts appear as terms); subject reduction for this system (Siles); explicit-implicit equivalence (EIE). : [[Well-Structured-Derivation-Trees|Link]]
- **3.4 About Well-Structured derivation trees** — silent substitution and silent derivation trees; a CTS variant tailored to well-structured trees, with its own inversion-on-product and substitution lemmas; "loud" and "whispering" derivation trees as intermediate notions. : [[Well-Structured-Derivation-Trees|Link]]
- **3.5 Future Work.** : [[Philosophy-and-Future-of-Interoperability|Link1]], [[STT-A-Constructive-Higher-Order-Logic|Link2]], [[Well-Structured-Derivation-Trees|Link3]]

**Key Questions:**
1. What does it mean for a derivation tree to be "well-structured," and what does that buy you — specifically, why does it license an induction principle compatible with $\beta$-reduction?
2. How does moving from an implicit (syntactic) to an explicit (semantic, cast-carrying) presentation of subtyping affect whether expansion postponement is provable?

### Chapter 4: Bi-directional CTS (pp. 101–112)

**Summary:** Splits ordinary CTS typing into an inference judgment (no subtyping) and a checking judgment (subtyping applied only at controlled points), defines the class of CTS specifications ("normal CTS") for which this bi-directional system is provably equivalent to ordinary CTS typing, and proves that equivalence. : [[Bi-Directional-Type-Systems|Link]]

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**
- **4.1 Presentation of bi-directional CTS** — bi-directional typing of CTS (Definition 4.1.1): $\Gamma \vdash t \Rightarrow A$ (inference) and $\Gamma \vdash t \Leftarrow A$ (checking). : [[Bi-Directional-Type-Systems|Link]]
- **4.2 Normal CTS** — CTS in normal form (Definition 4.2.1), the class of specifications for which the equivalence below holds. : [[Bi-Directional-Type-Systems|Link]]
- **4.3 Equivalence proof** — EBI (bi-directional embeddability); bi-directional typing soundness; check-to-infer and inversion lemmas (sort, product, sort-check); the main result, equivalence between typing and bi-directional typing (Theorem 4.3.9), which requires the derivation tree to be well-structured (linking back to Chapter 3). : [[Bi-Directional-Type-Systems|Link]]
- **4.4 Future Work.** : [[Philosophy-and-Future-of-Interoperability|Link1]], [[STT-A-Constructive-Higher-Order-Logic|Link2]], [[Well-Structured-Derivation-Trees|Link3]]

**Key Questions:**
1. Where exactly is subtyping/conversion allowed to fire in the bi-directional system, and why does restricting it there still preserve completeness with respect to ordinary CTS typing?
2. What does "CTS in normal form" require of a specification, and why is that the right sufficient condition for the equivalence theorem?

**Summary:** Generalizes PTS with an abstract, user-supplied conversion relation ("PTS modulo"), then specializes this to LF to obtain the $\lambda\Pi$-calculus modulo theory, and states the meta-theoretic properties (soundness, conservativity) that any embedding into it should satisfy. : [[Dedukti-An-Implementation-of-Lambda-Pi-calculus-Modulo-Theory|Link1]], [[STT-A-Constructive-Higher-Order-Logic|Link2]]

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**

- **5.2 Embeddings in $\lambda\Pi$-calculus modulo theory** — shallow encoding (Definition 5.2.1); the Cousineau & Dowek theorem: every functional PTS can be shallowly embedded into $\lambda\Pi$-calculus modulo theory. : [[Dedukti-An-Implementation-of-Lambda-Pi-calculus-Modulo-Theory|Link1]], [[STT-A-Constructive-Higher-Order-Logic|Link2]], [[Encoding-CTS-Into-LambdaPi-Calculus-Modulo-Theory|Link3]]

**Key Questions:**
1. How does "PTS modulo" generalize a Pure Type System with an abstract conversion relation, and in what precise sense is $\lambda\Pi$-calculus modulo theory just the LF instance of it?

### Chapter 8: Dedukti: An implementation of $\lambda\Pi$-calculus modulo theory (pp. 159–178)

**Summary:** Presents Dedukti, which recovers decidable type checking for $\lambda\Pi$-calculus modulo theory by orienting the abstract conversion as a (not-necessarily-confluent-or-terminating) term rewrite system, describes its concrete syntax and rewrite engine, and gives the Dedukti encodings of STT∀, of general CTS, and (at a high level) of inductive types. : [[Dedukti-An-Implementation-of-Lambda-Pi-calculus-Modulo-Theory|Link]]

- **8.4 Embedding inductive types in Dedukti** — inductive types, the `match` destructor, the fixpoint operator; the guard condition for soundness; encoding natural numbers as a worked example. : [[Dedukti-An-Implementation-of-Lambda-Pi-calculus-Modulo-Theory|Link]]

### Chapter 9: Rewriting as a Programming Language (pp. 179–196)

**Summary:** Presents Dkmeta, a tool that uses Dedukti's own rewrite engine as a meta-programming language for writing proof transformations, together with a quote/unquote mechanism that lets meta rewrite rules match constructs (products, syntactic applications) that Dedukti's pattern language cannot normally see. : [[Rewriting-as-a-Meta-Programming-Language-(Dkmeta)|Link]]

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**

- **9.2 Quoting and unquoting** — the `prod` quote function (products become patterns); the `lf` quote function (full syntactic pattern matching, prefixing every constructor); the `ltyped` quote function (adds type annotations to applications, requiring Dedukti's type checker). : [[Rewriting-as-a-Meta-Programming-Language-(Dkmeta)|Link]]

- **9.5 Dkmeta vs other meta-languages** — comparison with $\lambda$Prolog (Coq-Elpi), Beluga (contextual LF, correctness-focused), and Meta-Coq (certification-focused). : [[Cumulative-Type-Systems-(CTS)|Link1]], [[Rewriting-as-a-Meta-Programming-Language-(Dkmeta)|Link2]]
- **9.6 Future work** — a proposed generic quoting mechanism, and confluence/termination checking for meta rewrite systems. : [[Philosophy-and-Future-of-Interoperability|Link1]], [[STT-A-Constructive-Higher-Order-Logic|Link2]], [[Well-Structured-Derivation-Trees|Link3]]

### Chapter 10: Universo (pp. 197–210)

**Summary:** Presents Universo, an implementation of Chapter 2's embedding-decision algorithm for Dedukti: it elaborates a proof's sorts into fresh variables, uses an instrumented Dedukti type checker to generate the *free CTS* as a constraint problem, hands that problem to an SMT solver, and reconstructs a concrete, well-typed proof from the solution.

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**
- **10.1 Universo in a nutshell** — the four pipeline steps (elaboration, type checking/free-CTS generation, solving, reconstruction), worked through on a running example judgment.

### Chapter 12: Logipedia: An Encyclopedia of Proofs (pp. 221–234)

**Summary:** Having reached STT∀, this chapter exports the translated proofs to five systems (Coq, Lean, Matita, PVS, OpenTheory) with varying difficulty depending on how close each system's logic and proof representation is to STT∀'s, discusses the "concept alignment" problem this creates for the exported proofs, and presents Logipedia, the website that serves these exports.

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**
- **12.1 From STT∀ to Coq, Lean and Matita** — a direct sort-morphism to $C_3$ (Definition 1.5.12), since these three systems' CTS are supersets of it. : [[Exporting-Proofs-Across-Proof-Systems|Link]]
- **12.2 From STT∀ to PVS** — PVS as a conservative, sequent-calculus, predicate-subtyping extension of $\lambda$HOL; the difficulty of translating STT∀'s natural-deduction-style rules into PVS tactics without triggering PVS's automatic goal simplification, illustrated by a worked cut-heavy derivation.
- **12.3 From STT∀ to OpenTheory** — OpenTheory's ten-rule equality-only typing system; encoding STT∀'s $\Rightarrow$, $\forall$ and typed-$\forall$ connectives via $\top$/$\wedge$/equality (with derivations of introduction/elimination for each); removing $\beta$/$\delta$ steps by proving untyped and typed conversion equivalent (an easier case of the Chapter 3 problem, since STT∀ has no dependent types). : [[STT-A-Constructive-Higher-Order-Logic|Link]]
- **12.4 Concept alignment** — the problem of linking axiomatized, exported constants (e.g. `exp`, `nat`) back to a target system's real, defined library counterparts; roughly 40 constants and 80 axioms needed for the Fermat export into Coq. : [[Exporting-Proofs-Across-Proof-Systems|Link]]
- **12.5 Logipedia: An Online Encyclopedia** — taxonomy (type operator, parameter, definition, axiom, theorem); theory $Th(A)$ (Definition 12.5.1, the accumulated context a symbol depends on); main dependencies $Md(A)$ (Definition 12.5.2, dependencies not already implied transitively by others).
- **12.6 The website** — implementation (HTML/CSS/JS/PHP, MongoDB); pre-generating downloadable proof archives instead of computing them on demand.
- **12.7 Future Work** — exporting to HOL-Light; generalizing Logipedia beyond STT∀ to arbitrary logics (requiring a pretty-printer and a taxonomy per logic). : [[Philosophy-and-Future-of-Interoperability|Link1]], [[STT-A-Constructive-Higher-Order-Logic|Link2]], [[Well-Structured-Derivation-Trees|Link3]]

**Key Questions:**
1. Why is exporting to PVS harder than exporting to Coq, Lean or Matita, even though PVS's logic strictly extends STT∀'s?
2. What is "concept alignment," and why does translating through Dedukti's kernel-level encoding inherently produce proofs full of axioms that still need manual linking to a target library?

### Chapter 13: Conclusion (pp. 235–240)

**Summary:** Recaps the thesis's theoretical (Part I) and practical (Part II) contributions chapter by chapter, lists open conjectures and concrete extensions for each, and closes with a broader argument about why formal proof libraries lack the interoperability standards taken for granted elsewhere in computing, and what remains ("aligning" high-level, user-facing structure) beyond kernel-level proof translation.

**Key Definitions & Concepts by Section:**
- **13.1 Future Work** — a chapter-by-chapter list of open problems: canonical derivation trees for completeness (Ch. 2); the meaning of a derivation tree's minimal level (Ch. 3); whether every CTS specification is weakly equivalent to one in normal form (Ch. 4); using well-structuredness to prove injectivity of products (Ch. 5); a direct encoding bypassing bi-directional CTS (Ch. 6); extensions of STT∀'s polymorphism (Ch. 7); refiner-style extensions of Dkmeta (Ch. 9); scaling Universo's SMT-solving step (Ch. 10); generalizing the Matita→STT∀ pipeline (Ch. 11); recording structural metadata (implicit arguments, notations) for better Logipedia exports (Ch. 12). : [[Philosophy-and-Future-of-Interoperability|Link1]], [[STT-A-Constructive-Higher-Order-Logic|Link2]], [[Well-Structured-Derivation-Trees|Link3]]
- **13.2 Future of interoperability** — an analogy to standards in programming languages, networking and operating systems; the QED manifesto (1993–1996) as a historical, unsuccessful attempt at unifying formal mathematics; the claim that similar underlying logics explain why independently built libraries converge structurally; the remaining gap between kernel-level proof terms and the high-level languages (tactics, vernacular, notations) that libraries are actually authored in. : [[Philosophy-and-Future-of-Interoperability|Link]]

**Key Questions:**
1. What is the thesis's central explanation for why libraries built on different, independently developed proof assistants end up looking structurally similar — and why does that make CTS a good common framework for studying them?
2. Beyond kernel-level proof-term translation, what "last big piece" does the author identify as still missing for genuinely practical interoperability?
