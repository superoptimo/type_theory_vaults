# Extensions of Visibly Pushdown Automata and Their Limits

## The natural next question, and why it's tempting

[[Visibly-Pushdown-Automata]] got determinizability, full Boolean closure, and decidable universality/inclusion out of a single restriction: the input alphabet fixes which stack action every symbol performs. A single stack, though, can only model one kind of unbounded nesting at a time (one call stack). Real systems often have *two* independent sources of nesting — e.g., two communicating recursive processes, or a program with both a call stack and a separate data structure with stack discipline. The natural move is to generalize VPAs to **multiple stacks**, keeping the same "input symbol dictates the action" discipline on each stack independently.

This chapter is a cautionary tale about that natural move: two independently-proposed extensions in this direction — **2-visibly pushdown automata (2-VPAs / 2-OVPAs)** and a new class the chapter itself introduces, **visibly stack automata (VSAs)** — both turn out to break the one property that made VPAs valuable in the first place: determinizability. One of these results directly **refutes a published claim** in the prior literature. This is the thesis's second contribution, and its lesson generalizes well beyond these two specific automaton classes: extending a determinizable formalism by adding a second independent "channel" of visible structure does not, in general, preserve determinizability, even when each channel looks individually well-behaved.

## 2-visibly pushdown automata: visibility split across two stacks

A **2-pushdown alphabet** (Definition 4.8) is a pair of partitions $\Sigma^0 = \Sigma^0_c \cup \Sigma^0_r \cup \Sigma^0_i$ and $\Sigma^1 = \Sigma^1_c \cup \Sigma^1_r \cup \Sigma^1_i$ over the *same* alphabet $\Sigma$ — one partition governing stack 0, the other governing stack 1. A symbol can be, e.g., a push on stack 0 *and simultaneously* a pop on stack 1 — these are called **synchronized communication symbols** ($\Sigma^s_1 = \Sigma^0_c \cap \Sigma^1_r$, and symmetrically $\Sigma^s_0$). A 2-VPA (Definition 4.9) has transition families for push/2push/pop/2pop/internal/sync moves, each dictated purely by the input symbol, exactly mirroring the single-stack VPA discipline but doubled.

**Why this looks safe at first:** each individual stack still obeys visibility — you can always tell, from the symbol alone, what happens to *each* stack. The hope, made explicit in prior work by Carotenuto et al., was that this doubled visibility would carry over the same determinizability the single-stack case enjoys, just by running the Alur–Madhusudan-style congruence construction "twice."

**Expressiveness gain is real and striking** (Remark 4.5–4.6): 2-VPAs can accept $\{a^n b^n c^n \mid n \geq 1\}$, which is not even context-free — genuinely more expressive than a single VPA or PDA. But this power has a cost: **emptiness of general 2-VPAs is undecidable** (Theorem 4.7, by reduction from the halting problem for two-counter machines) — a serious departure from the single-stack world where emptiness was *always* the easy, polynomial-time question. To recover decidable emptiness, **ordered 2-VPAs (2-OVPAs)** (Definition 4.11) restrict pops on the second stack to only occur once the first stack is completely empty — an ordering constraint that makes emptiness solvable in $O(n^3)$ again (Theorem 4.8).

### Counter-example 1: why "run the determinization twice" doesn't work

The chapter's central technical contribution here is a detailed automaton, $M$, over 10 states accepting $L_1 = \{(ab)^i c^j d^{i-j} u x^j y^{i-j} v \mid i \geq 1, 1 \leq j \leq i\}$ — carefully engineered so that reading $(ab)^i$ interleaves pushes onto *both* stacks (first stack gets an $A$ or $B$ per $a$, second stack correspondingly on $b$), and $M$ nondeterministically "guesses," symbol by symbol, which of the $i$ pushed pairs will eventually be $j$ (matched by $c$/$x$) versus $i-j$ (matched by $d$/$y$).

The refutation argument is a clean pumping-style contradiction, structurally identical to how you'd disprove regularity with the pumping lemma, but targeted at *determinism* rather than *finiteness*:

1. Pick $i$ larger than the number of states of a hypothetical deterministic $M'$ recognizing $L_1$.
2. After reading $(ab)^i$, consider the states reached after each of $c^t d^{i-t}$ for $0 \le t \le i$ — by pigeonhole, two different values $j < k$ land in equivalent states $s_j \equiv s_k$ (the same control state *and* the same relevant stack content — because $c^t d^{i-t}$ only ever touches the *first* stack, the two stacks' contents after this phase can indeed coincide for different $t$).
3. But then appending the *same* suffix $x^j y^{i-j}v$ to both drives $M'$ to the *identical* configuration on words $w = (ab)^i c^j d^{i-j} u x^j y^{i-j} v$ (which is in $L_1$) and $w' = (ab)^i c^k d^{i-k} u x^j y^{i-j} v$ (which is not, since $L_1$ requires the $c/d$ split and the $x/y$ split to agree). A deterministic automaton reaching the same configuration must accept or reject both — contradiction.

**Why the original determinization proof missed this:** the claimed construction tracked one "summary edge" set per stack independently, implicitly assuming that reachability information from the two stacks composes cleanly. But interleaving actions across two stacks means a state's *history* on stack 0 (how many pushes are still open there) is entangled with what's happened on stack 1 in between — the two stacks' summary-edge sets don't stay independently sufficient to predict future behavior, precisely because visibility per-stack doesn't prevent the *global* run from correlating the two stacks in ways a per-stack subset construction can't see. This directly refutes the published claim that 2-OVPAs (and hence 2-VPAs) are determinizable.

## Visibly stack automata: visibility plus a movable read head

The second extension, introduced fresh in this chapter, generalizes VPAs along a different axis: instead of *adding a stack*, add a **movable stack-reading head** to a single stack — combining visibility with the older idea of *stack automata* (Ginsburg, Greibach, Harrison). A **visibly stack automaton (VSA)** (Definition 4.13) partitions $\Sigma$ into five kinds of actions: push, pop, internal (as before), plus **up** and **down** — moves that shift a reading head *within* the existing stack content without changing it, letting the automaton "reread" deeper parts of the stack it already built.

**Why this looks like a natural, conservative extension:** up/down moves don't add expressive stack operations in the way multi-stack extensions do — they just let the automaton *inspect* stack content it already committed to, and the stack automata literature already guarantees this class has a **decidable emptiness problem** (Corollary 4.9, inherited directly from the classical stack-automaton decidability result) and closure under union and intersection based purely on the visibility partition. It looks, on paper, like a strictly safer generalization than 2-VPAs.

### Counter-example 2: the same pumping argument, a different failure mode

The chapter shows VSAs are *also* not determinizable, via language $L_2 = \{a^i c^j d^{i-j} x^{i-j} y^j u v \mid 0 \le j \le i\}$: an automaton pushes $A$ then nondeterministically switches to a state that pushes $B$ instead, encoding a guess of $j$ directly into *where in the stack* the switch from $A$'s to $B$'s happens; then "down" moves count $c$'s against the $B$'s and $d$'s against the $A$'s, and "up" moves re-traverse the same content counting $x$'s and $y$'s in the opposite order to confirm consistency.

The refutation is the identical proof shape as Counter-example 1: after $a^i$, pigeonhole two positions $j < k$ into equivalent states via $c^t d^{i-t}$, then append a shared suffix $x^{i-j}y^j u v$ that's consistent with one guess but not the other, forcing a deterministic automaton into simultaneously accepting and rejecting. **The recurring mechanism across both counter-examples:** a nondeterministic guess made early (which pair index is the "split point") gets *confirmed* only much later in the word, and the confirming suffix can be replayed against a different, wrongly-guessed prefix that a determinized automaton was forced to conflate with the correct one. Whenever a formalism can defer verification of a guess this way while collapsing enough intermediate states via pigeonhole, determinizability fails — regardless of whether the deferred confirmation channel is a second stack (Counter-example 1) or a repositionable head on one stack (Counter-example 2).

## The deeper lesson: non-determinizable classes cannot be complemented, and inclusion becomes an open question

Section 4.3 draws out the sharpest consequence: both 2-OVPAs and VSAs **universally reject certain words purely due to their own definitional structure** (e.g., a specific fixed word like $a^3b^3xycdyd$ under Counter-example 1's partition can never be accepted by *any* 2-OVPA over that alphabet, deterministic or not, simply because of how the partition constrains transitions) — which means these classes are **not closed under complementation**: there's no way to build "the automaton for everything this class *can't* express" within the same class. Since the standard route to inclusion checking (complement one side, intersect, check emptiness) requires exactly this closure property, **the book explicitly leaves the inclusion problem for 2-OVPAs and VSAs open**, conjecturing (but not proving) undecidability.

This is a meaningful methodological point about the shape of decidability results in this area: refuting determinizability doesn't *immediately* settle inclusion — it only removes the one proof technique (complement-and-intersect) that would have made inclusion easy. Whether a genuinely different technique might still recover decidable inclusion for these extensions (the way [[Superdeterministic-Pushdown-Automata]] achieves one-sided inclusion for a class that also isn't symmetrically closed) is left as an open conjecture, not resolved.

**Related work note (Nowotka–Srba, Caucal):** the book connects this failure to two adjacent lines of research that try to recover *some* decidability without full visibility: height-deterministic PDAs (Nowotka–Srba) — a strictly weaker condition than visibility, requiring only that stack height be a function of the input word, not that push/pop be alphabet-determined — get decidable inclusion, but *only* when the two automata being compared are additionally **synchronized** (agree on stack height after every input); and Caucal's transducer-synchronized PDAs generalize VPAs into an effective Boolean algebra by synchronizing via a one-state integer-output transducer, of which ordinary visibility is the special case with outputs restricted to $\{-1, 0, +1\}$. Both are reminders that visibility (this chapter's failed extensions notwithstanding) sits at a genuine sweet spot — weaken it (height-determinism alone) and you need an extra synchronization side-condition; try to extend it directly (more stacks, a movable head) and determinizability itself collapses.

**A note for the `sat-smt-csp` focus area:** the refutation technique used in both counter-examples — pick a large enough instance, pigeonhole two nondeterministic guesses into an indistinguishable state, then exhibit a *concrete* distinguishing suffix that proves the guesses weren't actually equivalent — is the same shape of argument used to disprove overly optimistic abstractions in **CEGAR** (counterexample-guided abstraction refinement): an abstraction merges two concrete states because they look equivalent under the current abstraction, and a spurious counterexample (the "distinguishing suffix" here) is exactly what forces the abstraction to be refined. Here the "abstraction" being refuted is a *determinization claim* rather than a program abstraction, but the underlying proof pattern — merge-then-refute-via-a-concrete-witness — is structurally the same technique CEGAR loops use to search for the coarsest sound abstraction.

## Where this leads

This chapter is the thesis's evidence that VPA's "syntactic visibility" trick from [[Visibly-Pushdown-Automata]] is *fragile*, not a template that scales freely to richer stack disciplines — a genuinely useful negative result, since it redirects the search for decidable, richer-than-VPA inclusion procedures away from naive multi-stack or movable-head extensions. The thesis's own later positive extension, in [[Timed-Automata-and-Their-Pushdown-Extensions]], instead extends VPAs along an *orthogonal* axis (adding real-time clocks rather than more stack structure) — precisely because that axis, unlike the ones explored here, can be shown to preserve determinizability (via event clocks whose valuation depends only on the input word, never on nondeterministic choices).
